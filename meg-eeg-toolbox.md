MEG/EEG toolboxes are specialized software environments for preprocessing, analyzing, and visualizing magnetoencephalography (MEG) and electroencephalography (EEG) data. These toolboxes provide the computational infrastructure for extracting biomarkers from electrophysiological recordings, reconstructing source activity through inverse modeling, and characterizing brain dynamics in both resting-state and task-based paradigms. Within the ecosystem of whole-brain modeling, MEG/EEG toolboxes serve as critical bridges between experimental data and computational models, enabling the estimation of model parameters and validation of simulated dynamics against empirical observations.

## Motivation and Context

The analysis of MEG and EEG data presents unique computational challenges that generic signal processing packages cannot adequately address. Both modalities capture the electromagnetic fields generated by post-synaptic currents in cortical neurons, but they differ substantially in their physical properties: MEG is sensitive to tangential current sources and relatively unaffected by theconductivity of surrounding tissues, while EEG is sensitive to both radial and tangential sources and requires accurate models of head conductivity to achieve precise source localization. A robust MEG/EEG toolbox must therefore provide sophisticated forward modeling capabilities that account for these physical differences, along with preprocessing pipelines for artifact rejection, independent component analysis for source separation, and inverse solvers for estimating the underlying source distribution.

The development of MEG/EEG toolboxes accelerated dramatically in the early 2000s, coinciding with the availability of high-density recording systems and increased computational power. These tools transformed electrophysiology from a largely qualitative discipline into one capable of precise spatio-temporal mapping of brain activity. Today, MEG/EEG toolboxes are indispensable for research in cognitive neuroscience, clinical neurology, and brain-computer interfaces, and they play an essential role in parameterizing [[whole-brain-modeling]] frameworks such as [[the-virtual-brain]].

## Key Features

Forward modeling constitutes the foundational capability of any MEG/EEG toolbox. This involves computing the electromagnetic field distribution on the sensor array given a known source configuration in the brain. The most common approach uses the boundary element method (BEM) or finite element method (FEM) to solve the Poisson equation for the EEG problem, while MEG forward solutions typically employ spherical or realistic conductor models. Accurate forward models are essential for both source reconstruction and for coupling electrophysiological data to [[structural-connectivity]] matrices derived from diffusion imaging.

Inverse solvers transform the sensor-level recordings into estimates of brain source activity. These methods fall into two broad categories: distributed source models (such as minimum norm estimation, weighted minimum norm, or LOFT) that assign activity to each vertex in a source grid, and parametric models (such as dipole fitting or beamformers) that estimate a smaller number of equivalent current dipoles. The choice of inverse method involves trade-offs between spatial resolution, temporal resolution, and sensitivity to deep sources, and modern toolboxes typically provide multiple options to accommodate different research questions.

Connectivity analysis has become increasingly central to MEG/EEG research, particularly in the context of [[resting-state]] networks. Toolboxes provide various metrics for assessing functional connectivity, including coherence, phase-locking value, and Granger causality. These metrics can be computed in sensor space or in source space, the latter requiring prior inverse reconstruction. The resulting connectivity patterns can be compared against those derived from [[fMRI]] or used to constrain [[dynamic-causal-modeling]] frameworks.

## Relationship to TVB

[[the-virtual-brain]] leverages MEG/EEG data extensively for model parameter estimation and validation. The TVB framework includes dedicated adapters for importing preprocessed electrophysiological data, performing source reconstruction using the toolbox's forward and inverse solvers, and extracting empirical connectivity matrices that inform the [[personalized-brain-modeling]] pipeline. This integration enables researchers to fit whole-brain models to individual subject data, optimizing parameters such as coupling strength, conduction delays, and local dynamics to match observed [[brain-oscillations]] and functional connectivity patterns.

Beyond connectivity, MEG/EEG toolboxes provide the temporal dynamics necessary for validating simulated brain states. Empirical recordings exhibit characteristic spectral fingerprints—alpha rhythms, beta bursts, gamma oscillations—that must be reproduced by whole-brain models for them to be considered biologically plausible. ## Major Toolboxes

Several mature MEG/EEG environments serve the community. **FieldTrip** is a MATLAB toolbox developed at the Donders Institute that provides comprehensive routines for preprocessing, source reconstruction, and connectivity analysis, with particular strength in beamforming and time-frequency analysis. **EEGLAB** is a widely-used MATLAB toolbox focused on EEG processing with extensions for independent component analysis and artifact rejection. **MNE-Python** offers a Python-based alternative with excellent integration with scientific Python libraries, strong source reconstruction capabilities, and growing support for MEG/EEG connectivity. **Brainstorm** provides a graphical interface oriented toward ease of use, with sophisticated visualization tools and automated preprocessing pipelines.
