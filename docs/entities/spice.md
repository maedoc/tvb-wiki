Spice (/spaɪk/) is a multi-GPU, time-driven spiking neural network simulator written in C++ with CUDA acceleration [[raw/articles/spice-github.md|Spice GitHub]]. Originally developed by Dennis Bautembach during doctoral research at the University of Crete, the software targets high-performance simulation of large-scale networks of spiking neurons on graphics processing units [[raw/articles/bautembach-phd.md|Bautembach, 2021]]. Unlike simulators that ship with fixed neuronal model libraries, Spice allows researchers to define custom neuron and synapse models directly in native C++ through a concept-based application programming interface, making it adaptable to diverse computational neuroscience questions [[raw/articles/spice-github.md|Spice GitHub]].

## Performance and Architecture

The simulator achieves state-of-the-art performance through several optimization strategies that reduce memory traffic and synchronization overhead. Published benchmarks demonstrate sub-second network setup times and the ability to simulate ten seconds of biological time in under 1.5 seconds on a single GPU for networks comprising one billion synapses [[raw/articles/spice-github.md|Spice GitHub]]. These results are enabled by techniques including lazy event-driven plasticity updates, shared atomic operations for parallel spike delivery, and work-queue-based propagation, which together established Spice as among the fastest spiking simulators available at the time of publication [[raw/articles/bautembach-phd.md|Bautembach, 2021a]][[raw/articles/bautembach-phd.md|Bautembach, 2021b]][[raw/articles/bautembach-phd.md|Bautembach, 2020]]. For multi-GPU configurations, the simulator implements static load balancing that reportedly scales linearly across up to eight graphics cards, allowing network sizes to approach the regime relevant for whole-brain connectivity models derived from human tractography data [[raw/articles/bautembach-phd.md|Bautembach, 2021b]].

## Relationship to TVB

Spice occupies a methodological position adjacent to whole-brain modeling frameworks such as [[the-virtual-brain]] (TVB). TVB simulates large-scale primate brain network dynamics by coupling [[neural-mass-model]]s—population-level mean-field approximations of neuronal activity—via empirical [[structural-connectivity]] matrices derived from diffusion MRI tractography [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al., 2013]]. Because these neural mass models intentionally abstract away single-neuron spiking dynamics, detailed [[spiking-neural-networks]] simulators like Spice serve as complementary tools for validating the biophysical assumptions that underlie such coarse-grained descriptions. Recent advances in multi-scale co-simulation illustrate the growing importance of this bridge: the Arbor-TVB framework integrates the [[arbor]] spiking simulator with TVB, using an MPI intercommunicator to translate between discrete spikes from detailed regional populations and continuous mean-field activity at the whole-brain level in real time [[raw/papers/arxiv-2505.16861.md|Hater et al., 2025]][[raw/papers/semanticscholar-eb704b6f5462.md|Hater et al., 2026]]. Although Spice has not yet been integrated into [[tvb]] through such a formal co-simulation interface, its performance profile and flexible model definition make it a candidate platform for analogous workflows in which cerebellar or cortical microcircuits are embedded within global [[whole-brain-modeling]] dynamics.

## Related Software

Within the broader simulation landscape, Spice complements established tools such as [[nest]], [[brian2]], and [[neuron]]: where NEST excels at constructing large-scale networks from standardized cell models and NEURON at multi-compartment morphological detail, Spice prioritizes raw throughput and custom model flexibility on GPU hardware [[raw/articles/spice-github.md|Spice GitHub]]. Related GPU-accelerated alternatives include [[brian2cuda]] and [[annarchy]], which offer Python-fronted interfaces, whereas Spice requires native C++ model definitions. The simulator's modular architecture distinguishes stateless input-processing neurons, stateful neurons with internal dynamical variables, and external input populations, and supports both fixed-weight and plastic synapses implementing spike-timing-dependent plasticity [[raw/articles/spice-github.md|Spice GitHub]]. This design lets users move from detailed biophysical simulations of specific circuits to reduced population descriptions that can inform mean-field approximations used in whole-brain platforms.
