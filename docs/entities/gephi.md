## Overview

Gephi is an open-source interactive network visualization platform designed for exploring and manipulating graph-structured data across scientific domains. Within computational neuroscience, it serves as a dedicated environment for rendering brain networks derived from multimodal neuroimaging, complementing the computational toolboxes that construct and analyze these graphs.

## Motivation and Context

The connectomics field generates high-dimensional adjacency matrices that resist interpretation from numerical tables alone. As brain network analysis has matured, the need for accessible visualization has grown alongside formal graph-theoretic methods: measures such as modularity, clustering coefficient, and characteristic path length must be evaluated with careful attention to whether networks are represented as weighted or binary, directed or undirected [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. Review work has established that these representational choices fundamentally determine how connectivity patterns are interpreted, and that metrics computed by packages such as the [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]] require contextual inspection before biological conclusions are drawn [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. Comprehensive pipelines for brain connectivity analysis consequently integrate visualization as a core step, reflecting the principle that statistical summaries and interactive spatial layouts are jointly necessary for valid network inference [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]].

## Brain Network Visualization and Analysis

Gephi addresses this gap by importing adjacency matrices and rendering nodes and edges as manipulable objects within force-directed or custom spatial layouts. Researchers can filter edges by weight threshold, partition nodes by community structure, and map graph-theoretic properties onto visual attributes such as size or color. The platform handles networks derived from [[diffusion-imaging]] and [[tractography]]—which yield [[structural-connectivity]] matrices—as well as [[functional-connectivity]] graphs constructed from [[fmri]], [[eeg]], or [[meg]] time series, in each case preserving the distinction between weighted and binary, directed and undirected formulations that guide subsequent analysis [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. Because Gephi supports importing metrics computed externally, outputs from the [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]] or similar libraries can be overlaid directly onto the network layout, facilitating qualitative verification of [[small-world-networks]] properties, hub organization, and modular community structure [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. This visual workflow is especially valuable when connectomes are compared across imaging modalities or between empirical data and simulated outputs [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]].

## Relationship to TVB

Gephi functions as a complementary visualization layer for outputs generated by [[the-virtual-brain]], the open-source platform for simulating primate brain network dynamics [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. TVB constructs personalized whole-brain models by integrating empirical structural connectivity from diffusion MRI tractography with [[neural-mass-models]], then produces simulated neural activity that is compared against empirical recordings through forward models for [[fmri]], [[meg]], and [[eeg]] [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. These simulations yield anatomical connectivity weights and dynamic correlation matrices that can be exported for visual inspection, enabling researchers to verify that simulated topology recapitulates known features such as densely interconnected hubs and modular community structure before proceeding to quantitative statistical comparisons [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. This qualitative sanity check represents a critical step in the [[model-validation]] pipeline for [[whole-brain-modeling]] studies.

## Related Software

Multiple toolboxes occupy adjacent niches in the brain connectivity ecosystem. [[braph]] provides an open-source MATLAB pipeline for constructing, analyzing, and visualizing brain networks from multimodal neuroimaging data including [[fmri]], structural MRI, [[eeg]], and PET [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]]. The [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]] remains a widely used MATLAB resource for computing graph-theoretic metrics that are typically exported to dedicated visualization environments for subsequent interpretation [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. While comprehensive pipelines such as [[braph]] orient toward group-level statistical analysis, specialized visualization platforms like Gephi focus on interactive exploration of individual network topology, reflecting the diversity of methodological needs in the connectomics community [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]].
