## Abstract

The advancement of next-generation energy storage technologies is essential to meet the growing demands for efficient, high-capacity, and sustainable power systems. Traditional battery modeling techniques–such as purely physics-based or data-driven models–often fall short in capturing quantum-scale phenomena and fail to scale across different simulation layers. These limitations hinder the accurate prediction of material behavior under dynamic operational conditions. This study proposes a Quantum-Inspired Multi-Scale Modeling Framework that integrates quantum embedding techniques, atomistic simulations, continuum modeling, and machine learning surrogates to enhance battery design and analysis. The objective is to develop a unified, scalable approach capable of simulating complex material interactions from electronic to device levels. Novelty lies in coupling Density Matrix Embedding Theory (DMET), molecular dynamics (LAMMPS), finite element methods (COMSOL), and Graph Neural Networks to form a cohesive simulation pipeline. The framework demonstrates superior accuracy (99.8%) with high scalability and moderate computational speed, significantly outperforming traditional methods like PINN-surrogate P2D models or hybrid ML-SPM approaches. Key findings reveal improved predictions of energy density, charge rate, and stability, especially in quantum-enhanced materials. Validation against the OBELiX dataset confirms the model's robustness. Tools used include Quantum ESPRESSO, LAMMPS, COMSOL Multiphysics, and PyTorch-Geometric, supporting a modular and extendable simulation environment for advanced energy storage systems.
