## Abstract

Integrating machine learning with brain atlases to establish brain functional connectivity for aiding brain disease diagnosis is currently a research focus. However, most existing machine learning methods model the functional connectivity network of brain regions using a single brain atlas, which overlooks the complementarity between different brain atlases. Although multi-atlas methods can address this issue, traditional multi-atlas brain networks are usually directly constructed into a single joint graph structure. This may ignore the potential information interaction between different atlases and also amplify the potential noise in cross-atlas connections, ultimately leading to the loss of key pathological information in brain regions. Thus, we propose the Interactive Two-Stage GNN (IT-GNN) for MultiAtlas Brain Network Analysis, which comprises two modules: MFCC and GRLC. The MFCC module generates a joint graph by integrating information from two distinct brain atlases. The GRLC module adopts an interactive two-stage GNN architecture (incorporating intra-atlas and inter-atlas propagation) to capture the functional coupling relationships at similar spatial positions in the joint graph, thereby enhancing the expression of pathological information in similar brain regions. Experimental results show that our method outperforms other approaches in all metrics on the AMRD dataset. Additionally, in the visualization results, different brain templates exhibit extremely high similarity in lesion areas.
