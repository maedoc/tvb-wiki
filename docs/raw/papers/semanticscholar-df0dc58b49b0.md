## Abstract

Dyslexia, a common neurodevelopmental disorder, disrupts reading, language processing, and comprehension. Early identification is crucial for enhancing a good education and prompt intervention. The proposed study presents a fully automated system for dyslexia detection based on brain MRI scans and emerging deep learning methods. The model combines Vision Transformer (ViT) to produce high-level spatial features of MRI slices, a Deep Q-Network (DQN) to select features adaptively, and a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network for sequential classification across MRI slices. The preprocessing pipeline involves skull stripping, normalization, resizing, noise elimination, and augmentation of the input. The experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed system achieves an accuracy of 99.8 %, precision of 99.8 %, and $\mathbf{F 1}$-score of 99.75 %, which proves the effectiveness of the feature extraction technique in terms of the combination of transformer and reinforcement learning with sequential modeling. The framework is a scalable, precise, and lowsupervised solution to early screening in terms of dyslexia, wherein a basis of AI-aided neuroimaging analysis and possible clinical implementation is founded.
