## Abstract

The growth of weeds in cotton fields leads to significant crop yield losses, with increased weed growth occurring in areas between cotton plants. To manage this, it is crucial to classify the weeds to identify their presence and deploy automated machinery for removal. While Deep Learning techniques have been widely used for classification tasks, they come with the drawbacks of high energy consumption and substantial memory usage. Recently, Spiking Neural Networks have gained attention in image processing because of their compatibility with low-power neuromorphic hardware. This paper evaluates the performance of a Spiking Neural Network using the Integrate and Fire Neuron model to classify weeds in cotton fields. To increase computational efficiency, bilinear interpolation is employed during data preprocessing. A Pytorch-based library, Sinabs is used to convert a Convolutional Neural Network to a Spiking Neural Network. Three classes of cotton weed images from a publicly available source are used, and the results are presented in terms of accuracy, training time and neuron spiking output for sample images. The loss value for the network is 1.78. The accuracy values obtained for 3 classes of weeds are $84.47 \%$ for Convolutional Neural Network and $\mathbf{8 3. 3 4 \%}$ for converted Spiking Neural Network.
