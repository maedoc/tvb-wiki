## Abstract

Brain imaging is a critical tool in mental health diagnostics, particularly for detecting anomalies associated with behavioral disorders, anxiety, depression, and neuropsychiatric conditions. Traditional deep learning approaches, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), have shown promise in analyzing functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI), but they often suffer from computational limitations, feature extraction inefficiencies, and generalization issues. Quantum Machine Learning (QML) offers a transformative solution by enhancing feature representation, improving classification accuracy, and exploiting the power of quantum computation for large-scale medical imaging datasets. In this paper, we explored the potential of quantum models for detecting anomalies in brain imaging. Using the UCLA Consortium for Neuropsychiatric Phenomics dataset, we compared quantum models, including the Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC), Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (QCNN), and Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM), with classical models like CNNs and the Support Vector Machine (SVM). Our results revealed that QSVM and VQC outperformed classical models, both achieving 92% accuracy and perfect recall (100%), making them highly effective at identifying positive cases-a critical requirement in clinical diagnostics. QCNN, while less accurate at 74%, still showed strong precision (90%) and an F1score of 85%, highlighting its potential where avoiding false positives is key. Classical models, in comparison, performed moderately well: SVM reached 87% accuracy and CNN achieved 83%, but both had lower precision and F 1 -scores, suggesting less consistent performance. These findings suggest that quantum models, especially VQC and QSVM, offer a promising advantage in neuroimaging-based mental health diagnostics.
