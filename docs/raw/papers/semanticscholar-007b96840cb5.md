## Abstract

Time series analysis has emerged as a critical tool across diverse fields such as finance, energy, healthcare, and environmental studies, enabling researchers to model and forecast temporal data. This review provides a comprehensive examination of R’s capabilities for time series analysis, emphasizing its role as a preferred platform for both novice and expert analysts. The paper addresses key methodologies, including ARIMA, exponential smoothing, and GARCH models, alongside advanced tools for data preprocessing, visualization, and diagnostic testing. A systematic review of the literature and R’s ecosystem of packages was conducted, focusing on their application to real-world scenarios. The findings highlight R’s strengths in statistical modeling, visualization (e.g., ggplot2), and forecasting through packages like forecast and TSAF. Additionally, the paper identifies gaps in R’s scalability for big data, limited integration with machine learning frameworks, and comparative studies with tools like Python and MATLAB. This review underscores R’s versatility in handling time series data while offering actionable recommendations for practitioners and future research. Key takeaways include the importance of hybrid modeling approaches, leveraging R’s opensource libraries for domain-specific applications, and addressing limitations in big data and machine learning integration. The findings not only serve as a guide for practitioners but also outline opportunities for enhancing R’s ecosystem for time series analysis.
