## Abstract

Aiming at the prediction of thin reservoirs in the Mesozoic strata of the HQ Block in the Ordos Basin, an innovative multi-scale thin reservoir comprehensive prediction technology based on multi-information fusion has been developed. Based on a comprehensive analysis of lithofacies, log facies, and seismic facies, the large-scale reservoir prediction in extensive work areas and thick layers (member s) is conducted by utilizing forward modeling, seismic attribute analysis, multi-attribute optimization, and multi-information fusion of dominant attributes, aims to delineate the distribution of sedimentary subfacies, predict the general distribution pattern of sand bodies, and constrain small-scale results. By integrating petrophysical analysis, characteristic curve reconstruction, geostatistical co-simulation inversion, and MGI inversion, a multi-information fusion approach is applied to conduct small-scale reservoir prediction in limited work areas and thin layers (sand groups), clarifying sedimentary microfacies distribution and delineating spatial development characteristics of sand bodies within subdivided layers. This study innovatively applies a multi-attribute fusion technology based on multimodal learning and convolutional neural networks (CNN), which matches seismic reflection characteristics with lithology labels through machine deep learning, extracts kinematic and dynamic attributes of seismic waves, optimizes sensitive attributes and their fusion, achieving optimal consistency between reservoir prediction results and actual drilling data, with spatial distributions aligned with sedimentary patterns and geological understanding. The research achievements of this study have demonstrated exceptional application effectiveness in the HQ Block. Through the implementation of this technical series, multiple wells targeting the Mesozoic strata in the HQ Block have achieved commercial/high-yield oil flows, with significant expansion of the hydrocarbon-bearing area and reserve scale, effectively supporting the oilfield's exploration and development operations.
