## Abstract

Migraine is a common neurological disorder that substantially affects quality of life (QoL) and is associated with altered cortical activity. While advanced neuroimaging has enhanced understanding of migraine-related brain changes, such methods are often costly and not widely accessible. This study investigates the feasibility of using a low-channel, wearable electroencephalography (EEG) device to assess migraine severity in a non-clinical population of university students. Fifty-three participants were stratified into four groups based on the Migraine Disability Assessment (MIDAS) questionnaire. Resting-state EEG data were recorded with the Muse 2 headband and processed via EEGLAB in MATLAB. Power spectral density (PSD) analysis focused on alpha (α, 8–12 Hz) and beta (β, 13–30 Hz) activity in frontal and temporal regions. Although statistical analysis using one-way ANOVA did not yield significant differences, a decreasing trend in α and β power was observed with increasing migraine severity. Participants also completed the Migraine-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (MSQv2.1), which showed no significant differences among the groups. The findings support the feasibility of using consumer-grade EEG systems for non-invasive assessment of migraine severity and provide a foundation for future studies involving larger cohorts and advanced signal analysis.
