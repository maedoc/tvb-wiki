## Abstract

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) with visual attention mechanisms offer a biologically-inspired approach for medical image analysis and segmentation, aiming to address the high computational cost and power consumption of traditional deep learning models. This work proposes a SNN architecture that mimics the brain's efficient processing for tumor segmentation in brain MRI images. We explore spike-based attention mechanisms and compare them to conventional models, highlighting the potential for energyefficient, interpretable, and deployable solutions in medical imaging. Our contributions include the design of a spiking visual attention network, a comprehensive evaluation against standard architecture, and insights into the role of attention in spike-based segmentation. This advances the development of energy-efficient, biologically plausible neural networks for medical imaging applications.
