## Abstract

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) are a robust computer model that accurately replicates the temporal rhythms and synaptic activity of biological neurons, representing notable progress in the domain of neural computation. This paper examines the use of SNNs in simulating synaptic activity in large-scale brain simulations, focusing on their capacity to accurately reproduce the complex mechanisms of neuronal communication and plasticity. Unlike conventional neural networks, SNN s use accurate spike timing and temporal coding to replicate synaptic contacts. This facilitates a more authentic depiction of brain processes such as learning, memory, and signal transmission. This study investigates the key characteristics of spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) to illustrate the ability of SNNs to represent synaptic plasticity and adaptability in neural circuits accurately. It demonstrates with a sequence of simulations that SNN s accurately represent the complex synaptic dynamics and provide a valuable understanding of the fundamental processes involved in cortical processing. The results emphasize the potential of SNN s to enhance our comprehension of brain function and to construct advanced neurocomputational models for future study.
