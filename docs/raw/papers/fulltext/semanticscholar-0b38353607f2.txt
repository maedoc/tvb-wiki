        AND wMEM) FOR EPILEPTOGENIC ZONE LOCALIZATION IN EEG/MEG:
                       TOWARDS NEUROENHANCEMENT

    ABSTRACT
    Objective: In order to determine the most accurate approach for presurgical planning in
    pharmacoresistant epilepsy, the goal of this study is to examine the effectiveness of four
    distributed source localization techniques for identifying the Epileptogenic Zone (EZ) using
    MEG.

    Theoretical Framework: The inverse problem of source localization in neuroimaging serves as
    the foundation for the study. MEG-recorded gamma oscillations are used as biomarkers for
    epileptic activity in the theoretical framework, which is based on the principles of
    electrophysiology and validated against the clinical gold standard of intracranial EEG (iEEG).

    Method: Four inverse techniques are compared in the methodology: Maximum Entropy on the
    Mean (MEM), standardized Low-Resolution Brain Electromagnetic Tomography (sLORETA),
    Dynamic Statistical Parametric Mapping (dSPM), and Minimum Norm Estimation (MNE). Three
    metrics were used to quantitatively assess localization accuracy: active volume measurements,
    correlation analysis of source activities, and the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) for spatial
    overlap with iEEG networks.

    Results and Discussion: All methods localized parts of the seizure onset zone, according to the
    results. While dSPM offered wide network coverage and wMEM was excellent at detecting deep
    sources, sLORETA had the most compact active volumes and the highest concordance with iEEG
    (DSC=0.72). These results are interpreted in the discussion, emphasizing the differences in
    spatial spread and complementary strengths of each technique.

    Research Implications: Discussions of the research's theoretical and practical ramifications
    offer insights into how the findings may be used or impact clinical neurology and neurosurgery
    procedures. These ramifications might include better patient surgical outcomes, improved
    presurgical planning for epilepsy centers, and an improved computational neuroscience
    methodological framework.

    Originality/Value: By offering a unique, multi-metric comparative analysis of important inverse
    techniques that has been directly validated with intracranial EEG, this study adds to the body
    of literature. sLORETA's recognition as a superior tool for accurate EZ delineation, which can
    directly influence clinical decision-making and enhance the quality of life for patients with
    drug-resistant epilepsy, demonstrates the research's relevance and value. This approach
    directly contributes to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 3) by enhancing
    diagnostic precision and paving the way for improved treatment options for the millions of
    people worldwide suffering from pharmacoresistant epilepsy.

    Keywords: epileptogenic zone, magnetoencephalography, source localization, inverse
    problem, intracranial EEG, presurgical evaluation, Sustainable Development Goals (SDG).

    1 INTRODUCTION


           Noninvasive neuroimaging methods, such as magnetoencephalography
    (MEG), have become essential for delineating epileptogenic networks, providing
    millisecond-level temporal resolution and enhanced spatial precision compared
    to scalp EEG(Gross et al., 2001). In cases of pharmacoresistant epilepsy,
    accurately pinpointing epileptogenic zones (EZs)—the cortical areas that trigger
    seizures(Peled et al., 2001)(Hadriche et al., 2022) is dependent on the
    examination of pathological biomarkers like interictal spikes and gamma-band
    oscillations (30–80 Hz). The clinical relevance of these biomarkers is heavily
    influenced by the effectiveness of inverse problem solutions that reconstruct
    neural sources from MEG data(Baillet, 2017) .
           Various distributed inverse techniques, including Minimum Norm
    Estimation     (MNE)(Hauk,      2004),     standardized      Low-Resolution       Brain
    Electromagnetic Tomography (sLORETA)(Pascual-Marqui et al., 2002), Maximum
    Entropy on the Mean (MEM)(Sociotogique, 2004), and Dynamic Statistical
    Parametric Mapping (dSPM)(McDonald et al., 2009), each operate under
    different assumptions regarding source distributions. MNE, for instance, tends
    to favor shallow sources due to its L2-norm constraint (Lin et al., 2006), while
    MEM utilizes spatial sparsity to effectively address extended epileptiform
    discharges(Chowdhury et al., 2013). A thorough evaluation of these methods
    necessitates robust metrics: (1) Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC)(Zijdenbos et
    al., 1994) to measure spatial overlap with intracranial EEG (iEEG) references,
    (2) time-course correlation to evaluate temporal accuracy, and (3) active
    volume assessments(Eickhoff et al., 2012) to measure spatial spread.
           Connectivity analysis enhances the identification of EZs by examining
    network interactions. Phase-based metrics such as Phase Locking Value
    (PLV)(Lachaux et al., 1999) assess synchronization in gamma oscillations, while
    Granger Causality (GC)(Bressler & Seth, 2011) determines the directionality of

    spike propagation. It's important to note that the selection of inverse method
    can significantly alter connectivity estimates(Elbehy et al., 2024); for example,
    sLORETA’s smoothness constraint can lead to an exaggerated coherence
    between neighboring areas(Grova et al., 2006), and dSPM is the most effective
    at identifying connections between brain regions(Nawel et al., 2019).
           This study aims to fill three important gaps: (1) a systematic comparison
    of MNE, sLORETA, MEM, and dSPM using DSC, correlation, and active volume
    metrics; (2) validation against iEEG data in five patients with drug-resistant
    epilepsy; and (3) the enhancement of connectivity analysis pipelines for clinical
    application. Our results indicate that MEM outperforms the others in terms of
    DSC (0.68 ± 0.12) and correlation (r = 0.72), while sLORETA effectively reduces
    false positives (active volume: 1.2 cm³). These findings contribute to
    presurgical planning by quantifying the trade-offs between spatial accuracy and
    network specificity in MEG-based EZ localization.


    2 MATERIALS AND METHODS


    2.1 MATERIALS


           The signal processing and analysis were conducted using MATLAB
    R2016b (MathWorks Inc.), a computational platform that enables advanced
    processing     of     neurophysiological       data(Palmini       et    al.,     1995).
    The Brainstorm toolbox (version 3.4) was employed for source localization and
    connectivity analysis, leveraging its intuitive graphical interface for MEG/EEG
    data visualization(M. Darbas, 2022).
           This study analyzed MEG recordings from five drug-resistant epilepsy
    patients (3 males, 2 females; age range: 28-45 years) acquired at the Clinical
    Neurophysiology Department of La Timone Hospital (Marseille, France). All
    patients exhibited frequent interictal epileptiform discharges (spikes) and
    gamma oscillations (30-80 Hz), identified as key biomarkers for epileptogenic
    zone localization(Hadriche et al., 2021). Data were recorded using a 151-
    channel CTF MEG system (sampling rate: 1,025 Hz; bandwidth: 0.1-330 Hz)
    during eyes-closed resting state.

    3 METHODS


    3.1 SIGNAL PREPROCESSING


           The study employed an integrated preprocessing pipeline using
    Brainstorm (Jmail et al., 2011) , EEGLAB, FieldTrip, and MATLAB (MathWorks,
    Inc.) toolboxes. Following established protocols (Frikha et al., 2016), we
    implemented a dual-stage event selection process where an experienced
    neurophysiologist first visually identified epileptiform events (both spikes and
    oscillatory bursts) in the raw recordings. These events were then subjected to
    specialized filtering procedures to minimize artifacts and prevent spectral
    overlap between different activity types. For spike analysis, we applied a 10-
    45 Hz band-pass FIR filter to emphasize high-frequency components while
    attenuating slow-wave interference. Oscillatory events were processed using a
    narrowband 29 Hz filter to isolate specific rhythmic activity. Both filters were
    designed with identical performance specifications: 3% maximum passband
    ripple (Rp) and 30 dB stopband attenuation (Rs) to ensure consistent signal
    quality across all analyzed events. This filtering strategy allowed for
    independent examination of sustained oscillations within their characteristic
    frequency ranges.


    3.1.1 Source localization of MEG signal


           Forward Problem Solution :The forward modeling approach combined
    multiple computational techniques to create accurate head models for each
    participant. We implemented three principal numerical methods - Boundary
    Element Method (BEM), Finite Element Method (FEM), and Finite Difference

           Inverse Problem Approaches: To address the inherently ill-posed nature
    of MEG source localization, we evaluated four complementary distributed
    source imaging techniques. The inverse problem presents fundamental
    challenges because infinite source configurations can produce identical scalp
    potential distributions. Our solution strategy incorporated neurophysiological
    constraints and regularization methods to identify biologically plausible source
    distributions(Geweke, 2014)(Hadriche et al., 2021)(M. Darbas, 2022). Unlike
    traditional dipole fitting approaches that require a priori specification of source
    count, all implemented methods utilized fixed-position 3D source grids with
    regularization    parameters     to   stabilize   solutions    and    mitigate    noise
    effects(Bressler & Seth, 2011). This distributed source framework provided
    several advantages for epilepsy research, including simultaneous analysis
    oscillatory events, which represent distinct but complementary biomarkers of
    epileptic activity.
           The implemented inverse problem solutions all utilize a standardized 3D
    source space with predetermined dipole locations, requiring only appropriate
    regularization to mitigate noise interference and produce physiologically stable
    reconstructions. Unlike traditional dipole fitting approaches that mandate
    specifying source quantities a priori, these distributed techniques overcome
    this limitation through their fundamental mathematical formulations. Each
    method brings unique value to source imaging: MNE excels in computational
    simplicity and processing speed, dSPM provides enhanced statistical reliability
    through its normalization approach, sLORETA achieves superior spatial precision
    via its smoothness constraints, while MEM incorporates valuable anatomical

               For SOZ localization, combine sLORETA (optimized cortical precision)

        requires DSC filtering (>0.5) to suppress false positives from its large active

        5 DISCUSSION & CONCLUSION


               This study systematically evaluated four distributed inverse methods
        (MNE, sLORETA, dSPM, MEM) for localizing epileptogenic networks using both
        spiky and oscillatory biomarkers in pharmaco-resistant epilepsy. Three critical

     findings emerge: First, sLORETA consistently outperformed other methods,
     demonstrating the highest concordance with iEEG (72% node overlap) and
     lowest propagation delays (18–25 ms), validating its precision for both cortical
     spiky events (via cross-correlation/Granger causality) and oscillatory networks
     (via phase-based metrics). Second, all methods successfully identified
     components of the seizure onset zone (SOZ), but with distinct network
     topologies—MEM revealed focal deep activations while dSPM mapped broader
     propagation pathways. Third, directed connectivity metrics (GC for spikes, PTE
     for oscillations) significantly enhanced network complexity analysis, with GC
     strengthening spiky network edges and PTE refining oscillatory phase coupling,
     confirming their complementary value.
            These results address a key gap in epilepsy research by simultaneously
     analyzing both event types and connectivity classes. While directed/non-
     directed metrics altered network complexity, they preserved core SOZ
     identification,   suggesting     method-specific      rather    than    metric-driven
     differences. The strong agreement between MEG and iEEG networks
     (particularly for sLORETA) supports MEG’s prognostic utility in presurgical
     evaluation. Future work should: (1) validate these findings in larger cohorts, (2)
     integrate additional methods (e.g., eLORETA, ST-MAP) for multi-technique
     consensus, and (3) explore real-time applications using the identified optimal
     metrics (GC/PTE for temporal precision, DSC>0.5 for spatial validation). This
     multi-modal framework advances toward personalized surgical planning by
     reconciling MEG’s temporal resolution with iEEG’s spatial specificity.
