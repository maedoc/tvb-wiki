## Abstract

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) refers to a wide range of neurodevelopmental disorders characterized by difficulties with speech, nonverbal communication, repetitive habits, and social skills. Early detection of ASD is crucial to implementing effective therapeutic interventions. Traditional diagnostic techniques based on clinical observation are effort-intensive and lack objective biological markers. Recent advances in neuroimaging, particularly Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), have enabled a more objective diagnosis of ASD. In this work, a comprehensive diagnostic framework that utilizes multimodal MRI data from the publicly available Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange (ABIDE-I) dataset. The proposed method extracts structural, functional connectivity, and temporal features using a 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) based on the Automated Anatomical Labeling (AAL) atlas, and a temporal CNN, respectively. To address the site variability inherent in multisite data, apply data harmonization using the ComBat algorithm. Phenotypic metadata are integrated and fused with imaging features, and an XGBoost classifier is used for final prediction. Furthermore, we employ SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) analysis to provide model interpretability and highlight the most influential features for ASD detection. The proposed approach achieves a test accuracy of 95% and an average AUC of 0.96, demonstrating strong clinical relevance and robustness.
