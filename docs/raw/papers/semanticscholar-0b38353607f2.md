## Abstract

Objective: In order to determine the most accurate approach for presurgical planning in pharmacoresistant epilepsy, the goal of this study is to examine the effectiveness of four distributed source localization techniques for identifying the Epileptogenic Zone (EZ) using MEG.
 
Theoretical Framework: The inverse problem of source localization in neuroimaging serves as the foundation for the study. MEG-recorded gamma oscillations are used as biomarkers for epileptic activity in the theoretical framework, which is based on the principles of electrophysiology and validated against the clinical gold standard of intracranial EEG (iEEG).
 
Method: Four inverse techniques are compared in the methodology: Maximum Entropy on the Mean (MEM), standardized Low-Resolution Brain Electromagnetic Tomography (sLORETA), Dynamic Statistical Parametric Mapping (dSPM), and Minimum Norm Estimation (MNE). Three metrics were used to quantitatively assess localization accuracy: active volume measurements, correlation analysis of source activities, and the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) for spatial overlap with iEEG networks.
 
Results and Discussion: All methods localized parts of the seizure onset zone, according to the results. While dSPM offered wide network coverage and wMEM was excellent at detecting deep sources, sLORETA had the most compact active volumes and the highest concordance with iEEG (DSC=0.72). These results are interpreted in the discussion, emphasizing the differences in spatial spread and complementary strengths of each technique.
 
Research Implications: Discussions of the research's theoretical and practical ramifications offer insights into how the findings may be used or impact clinical neurology and neurosurgery procedures. These ramifications might include better patient surgical outcomes, improved presurgical planning for epilepsy centers, and an improved computational neuroscience methodological framework.
 
Originality/Value: By offering a unique, multi-metric comparative analysis of important inverse techniques that has been directly validated with intracranial EEG, this study adds to the body of literature. sLORETA's recognition as a superior tool for accurate EZ delineation, which can directly influence clinical decision-making and enhance the quality of life for patients with drug-resistant epilepsy, demonstrates the research's relevance and value. This approach directly contributes to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 3) by enhancing diagnostic precision and paving the way for improved treatment options for the millions of people worldwide suffering from pharmacoresistant epilepsy.
