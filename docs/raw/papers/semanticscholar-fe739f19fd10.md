## Abstract

Over the past few decades, artificial intelligence (AI) has gained momentum, which is mainly because of deep learning models that are implemented on GPUs and TPUs. These accelerators have the Von Neumann bottleneck and high-power consumption as limiting factors which do not facilitate the ability of data to flow between processing and memory units even when widely used. To overcome these limitations, neuromorphic computing, which is inspired by the brain, uses massively parallel architectures and event-driven neurons with spiking to implement a paradigm. The following paper provides a performance-based analysis of neuromorphic computing and its relationship with artificial intelligence. Neuromorphic processor systems currently being studied, including the IBM TrueNorth, Intel Loihi and BrainScaleS, are evaluated and tested on benchmark tasks such as gesture recognition and MNIST classification. Experimental evidence suggests that neuromorphic systems are able to retain competitive accuracy rates at power consumption of up to 30 times lower and latency of 2-3 times lower than deep neural networks on a GPU.
