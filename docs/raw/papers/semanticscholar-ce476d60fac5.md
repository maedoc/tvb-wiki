## Abstract

Over the past decade, exceptional progress has been made in deep learning and its applications to medical imaging including image analysis, disease classification, medical image segmentation, and a variety of other tasks. Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive cognitive disorder and the most common cause of dementia in the world. In this research, we present a lightweight hybrid classical-quantum framework leveraging quantum machine learning (QML) using the pennylane library. Consisting of classical convolutional neural network (CNN) and quantum circuits as quantum nodes wrapped in a torch layer of pytorch, the framework has a total of eight layers including the quantum layer, for the classification of Alzheimer’s disease stages from brain MRI images. Using this framework, the 4 -class Alzheimer’s MRI dataset is assessed. This dataset contains 6400 MRI images across four classes of disease stages, available in the Kaggle. The dataset has gone through the Pre-processing stage of augmentation to scale and balance the classes. The proposed hybrid framework achieves an accuracy of 96.26 % in classifying 4 stages of Alzheimer’s Disease, outperforming the classical deep learning models while utilizing substantially fewer parameters. The superior performance of the hybrid framework is compared with the performance of four other classical models: 1. VGG 2. Densenet 3. Enet and 4. Resnet. Grad-CAM (Explainable AI) is employed to assess the proposed framework’s focus on relevant brain regions and to validate its accuracy through consultation with a medical professional. By ensuring alignment between the framework’s attention and clinical expertise, this research enhances the potential for assisting medical professionals in accurately interpreting neurodegenerative disease states and thereby advancing the development of clinically applicable diagnostic tools. It also demonstrates the feasibility of adopting quantum machine learning in medical image analysis and medical AI.
