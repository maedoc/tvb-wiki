## Abstract

Early detection of neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's remains a significant challenge in clinical neuroscience. Chronological age alone is insufficient to capture variations in brain ageing; hence, brain age estimation has emerged as a valuable biomarker. This study introduces a hybrid Graph Attention Network-Convolutional Neural Network (GAT-CNN) framework that integrates spatial feature extraction via an sSE-ResNet1 backbone with topological modelling through Graph Attention Networks. The Brain Age Gap (BAG), defined as the difference between predicted and chronological age, is employed as an interpretable diagnostic biomarker. Unlike conventional models, the proposed framework combines spatial and topological features, leveraging Bagging in conjunction with classifiers such as Support Vector Machine, AdaBoost, and XGBoost for disease classification. An experimental evaluation using four publicly available MRI datasets (IXI, OASIS, ADNI, and PPMI) demonstrates the superiority of this approach, achieving a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.867, a Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) of 0.999, and disease classification accuracy exceeding 91 %. These results highlight the novelty of combining graph-based and convolutional representations with BAGdriven classification, offering a reliable and interpretable tool for early detection of neurodegenerative diseases.
