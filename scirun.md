# SCIRun

## Overview

SCIRun (SCI + Run: derived from the Scientific Computing and Imaging Institute, with "Run" referring to the executable runtime environment) is an interactive problem-solving environment designed specifically for forward and inverse problems in bioelectric field simulation. Developed primarily at the University of Utah's Scientific Computing and Imaging Institute (SCI), SCIRun provides a modular, dataflow-based framework for constructing, manipulating, and solving complex computational models of electromagnetic fields generated by neural activity. The software integrates mesh generation, finite element method (FEM) solvers, inverse estimation algorithms, and advanced visualization capabilities into a single, flexibly configurable environment. Unlike general-purpose simulation platforms, SCIRun is architected around the specific demands of bioelectric phenomena, making it particularly well-suited for modeling the electric fields produced by post-synaptic potentials and action potentials that propagate through the brain's conductive tissue.

## Motivation and Context

The fundamental challenge in interpreting electrophysiological measurements such as electroencephalography (EEG) and magnetoencephalography (MEG) lies in the so-called inverse problem: given measurements of electric potentials at the scalp or magnetic fields outside the head, one must estimate the underlying neuronal sources that generated these signals. This inverse problem is mathematically ill-posed—there exist multiple source configurations that produce identical external measurements—and requires accurate forward models to constrain the solution space. Forward modeling involves computing the electric potential distribution (or magnetic field distribution) that results from a known configuration of current sources in a geometrically and electrically realistic head model. SCIRun emerged in the early 1990s to address the computational infrastructure needs of this research community, providing standard implementations of the mathematical models governing volume conduction and source localization that could be compared and validated across laboratories. Its development coincided with the growing availability of high-resolution anatomical MRI and the establishment of the field of quantitative electroencephalography, creating demand for standardized, reproducible forward modeling tools.

## Technical Framework

SCIRun's architecture centers on a visual programming interface in which computational workflows are constructed by connecting modular units called "modules" through a visual representation of data flow. Each module performs a specific operation—mesh creation, boundary element method (BEM) or finite element method (FEM) solving, source assignment, matrix inversion, or visualization—and the entire network represents the complete computational pipeline from imaging data to forward solutions or inverse estimates. The forward simulation capabilities in SCIRun implement the quasistatic approximation of Maxwell's equations, which is valid at the low frequencies (< 100 Hz) characteristic of neural activity. The electric potential φ at position r in a volume conductor with conductivity tensor σ(r) satisfies the Poisson equation ∇·(σ(r)∇φ) = -I_δ, where I represents the primary current source distribution. Analytical solutions exist only for simple geometries (spherical head models), so SCIRun provides numerical implementations including the boundary element method for piecewise constant conductivity (skin, skull, cerebrospinal fluid, brain) and the finite element method for piecewise linear or constant conductivity distributions with anisotropic white matter conductivities derived from diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) data.

## Relationship to The Virtual Brain

SCIRun and [[the-virtual-brain]] (TVB) address complementary aspects of whole-brain modeling. While SCIRun focuses on the electromagnetic forward problem—the precise computation of how currents flow through the head's conductive medium given known sources—TVB operates at the level of neural mass and neural field models that generate the sources themselves. In the TVB framework, the activity of large-scale brain networks is simulated using simplified population dynamics, and the resulting source distributions can be passed to SCIRun for forward projection to the scalp, enabling direct comparison with empirical EEG or MEG recordings. This separation of concerns reflects a principled approach to multi-scale modeling: TVB handles the biophysical and dynamical generation of neural activity at the level of cortical columns and brain regions, while SCIRun handles the physics of volume conduction that mediates the relationship between cortical sources and extracranial measurements. The software [[tvb-adapters]] provides integration modules that allow TVB simulations to be exported in formats compatible with SCIRun's forward solvers, facilitating this combined workflow.

## Key Features

The modular architecture distinguishes SCIRun from earlier monolithic packages for EEG/MEG forward modeling. Researchers can swap different head models (three-shell spherical, four-shell with skull anisotropy, individualized FEM meshes from structural MRI), different source models (single dipoles, distributed dipole meshes, current density fields), and different numerical methods (BEM, FEM) without changing the overall computational structure. Inverse problem modules implement several established algorithms including the weighted minimum norm estimate (wMNE), low-resolution electromagnetic tomography (LORETA), and beamforming approaches. Visualization modules enable real-time inspection of isopotential maps, current flow lines, source reconstructions, and anatomical overlays, facilitating the interpretation of complex simulation results. The software also includes mesh generation capabilities for constructing high-quality tetrahedral meshes from segmented MRI data, which are essential for accurate FEM forward calculations.

## Relationship to Other Software

SCIRun occupies a unique niche compared to other computational neuroscience tools in the wiki. While packages like [[mne-python]] and [[fieldtrip]] provide comprehensive pipelines for EEG/MEG analysis including forward modeling, they typically rely on simplified head models and emphasize statistical analysis of empirical data over the detailed numerical simulation that SCIRun enables. The boundary element method implementations in SCIRun are closely related to those in [[openmeeg]], another open-source forward solver, though SCIRun's visual programming paradigm differs substantially from OpenMEEG's command-line interface. In the context of whole-brain simulators, SCIRun is most analogous to the forward modeling components of [[tvb-multiscale]], which can interface with specialized solvers for electromagnetic fields. For visualization, SCIRun shares conceptual similarities with [[3d-slicer]] and [[paraview]], both of which originated at or influenced the University of Utah's visualization research ecosystem.

## Key Contributors and Development History

The SCIRun project was initiated by Dr. Robert MacLeod and Dr. David Weinstein at the University of Utah in the early 1990s, with substantial contributions from the Scientific Computing and Imaging Institute over subsequent decades. The software has been cited in numerous biomedical engineering and computational neuroscience applications, particularly in the epilepsy modeling community where accurate source localization is critical for pre-surgical planning. Development continues through the NIH-funded MIDAS (Medical Image Data Archive) and related initiatives, with the software maintaining compatibility with modern neuroimaging data standards including [[bids]] and integration pathways to tools like [[datalad]] for data management.
