## Overview

eConnectome is an open-source MATLAB toolbox for mapping, imaging, and analyzing brain [[functional-connectivity]] and [[effective-connectivity]] from electrophysiological recordings. Developed at the University of Minnesota, the toolbox integrates cortical [[source-localization]] with a suite of connectivity estimators — spanning spectral coherence, phase synchrony, Granger causality, and adaptive multivariate autoregressive models — into a unified graphical workflow. Its design goal is to reduce the technical barrier between raw EEG/ECoG/MEG sensor data and a spatially resolved, three-dimensional depiction of brain network interactions, making it a practical instrument for investigating large-scale [[network-dynamics]] in both [[resting-state]] and task-evoked paradigms.

## Motivation and Design

Electrophysiological brain mapping faces a persistent methodological gap: sensor-space recordings must first be projected onto the cortical surface via a [[forward-model]] before the resulting source waveforms can be interrogated for connectivity. Researchers historically had to chain together separate packages for head model construction, inverse solution, pairwise coupling estimation, and network visualization — a brittle pipeline that demands fluency in multiple toolchains and risks compounding errors at each handoff. eConnectome addresses this by providing a single integrated environment that carries the user from digitized electrode positions and structural MRI through boundary element method (BEM) head modeling, distributed source reconstruction, frequency-domain and time-domain connectivity estimation, and interactive three-dimensional rendering of the resulting cortical networks.

The toolbox adopts a modular architecture organized around four processing stages: (1) cortical imaging from scalp or intracranial recordings, (2) functional connectivity estimation via spectral coherence and phase locking, (3) effective connectivity estimation via the directed transfer function, partial directed coherence, and time-varying adaptive multivariate autoregressive models, and (4) network visualization and graph-theoretic analysis. This staged pipeline reflects a deliberate epistemological stance — that functional and effective connectivity are distinct, complementary descriptions of brain interaction that merit separate computational treatment — and makes that distinction operationally transparent to the researcher.

## Key Features and Connectivity Methods

eConnectome's cortical source imaging module constructs subject-specific BEM head models from structural MRI and coregisters electrode positions to solve the EEG forward problem, yielding distributed current density estimates on a triangulated cortical surface. Once source waveforms are obtained, the functional connectivity module provides spectral coherence, imaginary coherence (which suppresses volume-conduction artifacts), and phase locking value computed across user-defined frequency bands. The effective connectivity module implements the directed transfer function (DTF) and partial directed coherence (PDC) — frequency-domain Granger-causal measures derived from multivariate autoregressive model coefficients — alongside time-varying variants that track dynamic coupling changes via Kalman-filter or sliding-window adaptive estimation. These directed measures are essential for distinguishing driver-response relationships in [[brain-oscillations]] that spectral coherence alone cannot resolve.

For network-level interrogation, eConnectome computes standard [[graph-theory]] metrics — degree, clustering coefficient, characteristic path length, betweenness centrality, and small-world indices — and renders them as color-mapped cortical surface visualizations. The three-dimensional rendering engine supports interactive rotation, thresholding, and node-edge display, bridging the gap between abstract adjacency matrices and anatomically interpretable network topography.

## Comparison and Context

eConnectome occupies a specific niche in the ecosystem of electrophysiological connectivity tools. Unlike [[brainstorm]], which emphasizes MEG/EEG sensor-space processing and distributed source modeling with a broader general-purpose scope, or the [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]], which provides a comprehensive library of graph-theoretic measures but does not include source imaging, eConnectome tightly couples source-space reconstruction with directed connectivity estimation in a single MATLAB environment. Its emphasis on adaptive, time-varying effective connectivity distinguishes it from purely static connectivity pipelines. The toolbox has been applied in studies of seizure propagation in [[epilepsy-modeling]] and motor imagery brain-computer interfaces, where time-resolved directional coupling is critical for tracking dynamic network reorganization.

## Relationship to TVB

eConnectome and [[the-virtual-brain]] operate at complementary levels of the brain network hierarchy. eConnectome estimates connectivity empirically from recorded electrophysiological signals — it measures the statistical interdependencies and directed influences that actually occurred in a recording session. TVB, by contrast, *simulates* the network dynamics that emerge when a [[structural-connectivity]] scaffold is populated with [[neural-mass-models]], generating predictions that can be tested against empirical data. A plausible integrative workflow would use eConnectome-derived effective connectivity patterns as an empirical target for TVB parameter fitting: the directed coupling strengths and frequency profiles estimated by DTF or PDC could serve as constraints when tuning the coupling parameters and conduction delays of a [[whole-brain-modeling]] simulation. Conversely, TVB simulations incorporating [[epileptor]] or [[jansen-rit]] dynamics could generate synthetic EEG that is then fed through eConnectome's connectivity estimators, enabling a closed-loop validation where the accuracy of connectivity reconstruction methods is assessed against a known ground-truth network. This bidirectional relationship — eConnectome as empirical estimator and TVB as generative model — exemplifies the broader integration of data-driven [[connectomics]] with mechanistic computational neuroscience.
