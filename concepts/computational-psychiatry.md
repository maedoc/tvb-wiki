Computational psychiatry applies quantitative models from [[computational-neuroscience]] and [[whole-brain-modeling]] to understand the mechanisms underlying mental illness and predict individual treatment responses. Rather than describing psychiatric disorders solely through symptom checklists, the field seeks to link synaptic-level perturbations to distributed [[brain-network]] dynamics via mechanistic simulation. [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-ff8218c1e55e.md|Xia et al. (2026)]] constructed intervention-capable digital twin brains that integrate individual neuroanatomy and task-evoked dynamics, demonstrating that modulation of [[excitation-inhibition-balance]] produces bidirectional, heterogeneous network responses across individuals and predicts longitudinal symptom trajectories. These digital twin predictions were independently validated by pharmacological [[fmri]] data, establishing whole-brain models as experimental platforms for mechanistic perturbation and behavioral prediction [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-ff8218c1e55e.md|Xia et al. (2026)]]. Complementing this framework, [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-f52da2a6cbf2.md|Hofsähs et al. (2026)]] used [[the-virtual-brain]] with the [[jansen-rit-model]] to show that specific GABAergic deficits—altered inhibitory synaptic decay rates and reduced inhibitory synapse counts—account for increased transcranial magnetic stimulation evoked potentials, bridging cellular-scale inhibition to macroscopic [[eeg]] signatures in major depressive disorder and highlighting how whole-brain simulations can identify therapeutic targets [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-f52da2a6cbf2.md|Hofsähs et al. (2026)]].

Beyond biophysical simulation, the field also leverages [[machine-learning]] pipelines to model psychological states at scale and interact with complex human behavior through computational means. [[raw/papers/arxiv-2510.21720.md|Pareek (2025)]] presents a comprehensive, multi-component AI framework that bridges isolated predictive modeling with interactive generative dialogue, encompassing classical machine learning benchmarks, fine-tuned transformer models stabilized for regression tasks, and a parameter-efficient large language model functioning as an interactive "Personality Brain." This holistic research-to-deployment pipeline demonstrates how artificial intelligence can democratize large-scale psychological research and integrate predictive analysis with generative dialogue [[raw/papers/arxiv-2510.21720.md|Pareek (2025)]]. The convergence of such data-driven approaches with biophysical modeling frameworks like those of [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-ff8218c1e55e.md|Xia et al. (2026)]] highlights a growing recognition that computational psychiatry must span both mechanistic simulation and scalable statistical learning to achieve clinically actionable insight. Together, these strands illustrate how the field moves from descriptive correlation toward mechanistic prediction and [[personalized-brain-modeling|personalized intervention]], uniting network dynamics, synaptic physiology, and artificial intelligence in the study of mental illness.
