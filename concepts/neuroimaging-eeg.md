# EEG / MEG

**Electroencephalography (EEG)** and **Magnetoencephalography (MEG)** are non-invasive techniques for measuring brain electrical activity with millisecond temporal resolution.

## Overview

EEG records voltage fluctuations from ionic currents in neurons via scalp electrodes. MEG records magnetic fields generated by these currents using superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs).

Advantages over [[fmri]]:
- **Millisecond temporal resolution** (vs. seconds for [[bold-signal|BOLD]])
- Direct measurement of neural activity (vs. hemodynamic proxy)
- Excellent for studying [[brain-oscillations]] and fast dynamics

Limitations:
- Poor spatial resolution (requires source localization)
- [[volume-conduction]] makes source reconstruction ill-posed
- Sensitive to artifacts (eye blinks, muscle activity, head movement)

## Relationship to TVB

EEG/MEG provide critical validation for TVB at the temporal dynamics level:
- TVB can simulate source-localized EEG/MEG time series via forward models
- [[neural-mass-models]] in TVB generate oscillatory dynamics comparable to empirical EEG/MEG spectra
- TVB's [[jansen-rit-model]] and [[wendling-model]] were originally derived from EEG phenomenology
- TVB-[[mne-python]] integration enables direct comparison of simulated and empirical sensor-level signals
