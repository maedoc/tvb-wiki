# Epileptor Resting State

An extension of the Epileptor model incorporating stochastic dynamics for resting-state and interictal activity simulation.

## Overview

The EpileptorRS extends the standard Epileptor with:
1. Multiplicative noise on slow variables
2. Metabolic/energy considerations
3. Enhanced parameter ranges for interictal dynamics

## Key Modifications

### Stochastic Terms

Added noise to slow permittivity variable z:
```
dz = deterministic_terms + σ_z·ξ(t)
```

## Applications

- Interictal spike generation
- Sleep modeling
- Long-term monitoring
- [[seizure-prediction]]

# Delay-constrained re-entry governs large-scale brain seizures and other network pathologies

**Source**: arxiv
**ID**: 2508.04824
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04824
**Date**: 2025-08-06
**Year**: 2025
**Authors**: Paul Triebkorn, Huifang E. Wang, Marmaduke Woodman, Maxime Guye, Fabrice Bartolomei, Viktor Jirsa
**Categories**: q-bio.NC

## Abstract

Re-entry of travelling excitation loops is a long-suspected driver of human seizures, yet how such loops arise in patient brain networks -- and how susceptible they are to targeted disruption -- remains unclear. We reconstruct a millimetre-scale virtual brain from diffusion MRI of a drug-resistant epilepsy patient, embed excitable Epileptor neural fields, and show that realistic cortico-cortical delays are sufficient to generate self-sustaining re-entry. Systematic parameter sweeps reveal a narrow delay-coupling window that predicts oscillation frequency and seizure duration across 184 recorded seizures. Precisely timed biphasic stimuli or sub-millimetre virtual lesions abort re-entry in silico, yielding phase-dependent termination rules validated in intracranial recordings. Our framework exposes delay-constrained re-entry as a generic dynamical mechanism for large-scale brain synchrony and provides a patient-specific testbed for precision neuromodulation and minimally invasive disconnection.

# Large-scale brain dynamics of seizures: asymptotic analysis of a neural field model

**Authors**: Michael Breakspear, John A. Roberts, John R. Terry, Stefano Rodrigues, Nader Mahmud, Philip Robinson  
**Published**: 2006  
**Journal**: Journal of Computational Neuroscience  
**DOI**: 10.1007/s10827-006-8135-2

## Summary

Extension of neural field theory to model primary generalized seizures using a spatially distributed version of the Jansen-Rit model. Combines local columnar dynamics with long-range connectivity to capture seizure propagation.

## Key Contributions

- Spatially distributed Jansen-Rit model (neural field variant)
- Asymptotic analysis reducing high-dimensional model to low-dimensional dynamics
- Connection of seizure dynamics to bifurcation topology
- Framework for large-scale seizure modeling in whole-brain context

## Clinical Relevance

Models absence seizures (3 Hz spike-wave) and tonic-clonic transitions through parameter changes that shift the system through different bifurcation regimes—directly relevant to clinical seizure understanding.
