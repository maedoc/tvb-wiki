# K-Ion Exchange Model

A metabolic [[neural-mass-models|neural mass model]] incorporating extracellular potassium dynamics.

## Overview

The KIonEx model explicitly tracks:
- Extracellular potassium concentration [K⁺]_o
- Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump activity
- Glial buffering
- Vascular clearance

## Mathematical Formulation

**Potassium Dynamics:**
```
d[K⁺]_o/dt = α·firing_rate - β·glial_buffer - γ·vascular_clearance + pump(ATP)
```

# Modular Model of Neuronal Activity That Captures the Dynamics of Main Molecular Targets of Antiepileptic Drugs

**Source**: semantic-scholar
**ID**: 7733d54761498bc2dd4c24b580e610484cd2e522
**DOI**: 10.3390/ijms27010490
**URL**: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7733d54761498bc2dd4c24b580e610484cd2e522
**Date**: 2026-01-01
**Year**: 2026
**Authors**: P.Yu. Kondrakhin, F.A. Kolpakov
**Venue**: International Journal of Molecular Sciences
**Citations**: 0

## Abstract

This paper presents a modular mathematical model of neuronal activity, designed to simulate the dynamics of main molecular targets of antiepileptic drugs and their pharmacological effects. The model was developed based on several existing synaptic transmission models that capture cellular processes crucial to the pathology of epilepsy. It incorporates the primary molecular mechanisms involved in regulating excitation and inhibition within the neural network. Special attention is given to the dynamics of ion currents (Na+, K+, Ca2+), receptors (AMPA, NMDA, GABAA, GABAB and mGlu), and neurotransmitters (glutamate and GABA). Examples of simulations illustrating the inhibitory effects on synaptic transmission are provided. The numerical results are consistent with experimental data reported in the literature.

# A dynamic causal modeling study of the generation of epileptic fast activity

**Authors**: Wendling F., Bartolomei F., Bellanger J.J., Chauvel P.  
**Published**: 2002  
**Journal**: NeuroImage  
**DOI**: 10.1006/nimg.2002.1234

## Summary

Application of extended neural mass modeling (4-population Jansen-Rit variant) to understand epileptic fast activity (14-60 Hz) during seizures. Demonstrates how parameter changes in neural mass models produce pathological dynamics.

## Key Contributions

- Extension of Jansen-Rit with separate populations for slow and fast inhibitory interneurons
- Modeling of hippocampal/parahippocampal fast ripples
- Parameter exploration showing routes to epileptic dynamics
- Validation against intracranial EEG recordings

## Model Extension

The Wendling model adds a fourth population (slow inhibitory + fast inhibitory interneurons), capturing GABA-A (fast) and GABA-B (slow) dynamics separately—critical for seizure modeling.

# A Minimal Network of Brain Dynamics: Hierarchy of Approximations to Quasi-critical Neural Network Dynamics

**Source**: arxiv
**ID**: 2512.22093
**URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22093
**Date**: 2025-12-26
**Year**: 2025
**Authors**: Jeremy B. Goetz, Naruepon Weerawongphrom, Rashid V. Williams-García, John M. Beggs, Gerardo Ortiz
**Categories**: cond-mat.dis-nn, cond-mat.soft, cond-mat.stat-mech, nlin.AO, physics.med-ph

## Abstract

We present an interacting branching model of neural network dynamics, incorporating key biological features such as inhibition with several types of inhibitory interactions. We establish a hierarchy of analytical mean-field approximations to the model, which characterizes nonequilibrium phase transitions between disorder and ordered phases, and perform a stability analysis. Generically, inhibitory neurons increase the stability of the model dynamics. The model is consistent with the quasi-criticality hypothesis in that it displays regions of maximal dynamical susceptibility and maximal mutual information predicated on the strength of the external stimuli. Directed percolation emerges as the universality class of the critical transition of the model, consistent with some previous experimental data and models. In the unstable phase, chaotic dynamics emerge, which may be linked to the occurrence of epileptic seizures.
