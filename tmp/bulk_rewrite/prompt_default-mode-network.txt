# Default Mode Network (DMN)

The default mode network is a large-scale [[brain-network]] that is active when the brain is at wakeful rest and deactivated during externally-oriented tasks. It is involved in internally-directed cognition including self-referential processing, mind-wandering, and episodic memory.

## Definition

The DMN comprises brain regions that show:
- **High metabolism** during rest
- **Deactivation** during goal-directed tasks
- **Strong functional connectivity** during resting-state
- **Involvement** in internally-directed cognition

## Core Anatomy

### Key Regions
1. **Posterior Cingulate Cortex (PCC)** – Hub region
2. **Medial Prefrontal Cortex (mPFC)** – Self-referential processing
3. **Angular Gyrus** – Memory retrieval
4. **Hippocampus** – Episodic memory
5. **Lateral Temporal Cortex** – Semantic processing

### Connections
- Dense anatomical connectivity within network
- Hub organization with PCC as central node
- Connections to memory and emotional systems

## Functional Organization

### DMN Subsystems (Andrews-Hanna et al., 2010)

The DMN comprises two distinct subsystems:

1. **Dorsal Medial Prefrontal Subsystem**
   - Regions: dmPFC, TPJ, temporal pole
   - Function: Self-relevant mentalizing, social cognition
   - Task: Thinking about others' mental states

2. **Medial Temporal Lobe Subsystem**
   - Regions: Hippocampus, parahippocampus, vmPFC
   - Function: Episodic memory, future simulation
   - Task: Remembering past, imagining future

### Functional Role
- **Internally-directed cognition**: Mind-wandering, daydreaming
- **Self-referential processing**: Thinking about oneself
- **Episodic memory**: Past and future thinking
- **Theory of mind**: Understanding others
- **Moral reasoning**: Ethical judgments

## Relationship with Task-Positive Networks

### Anticorrelation
- DMN is anticorrelated with task-positive networks
- Strong negative correlation with executive networks
- Anticevic et al. (2012): DMN suppression facilitates task performance

### Dynamic Interactions
- Networks fluctuate in anti-phase
- Balance between internal and external focus
- Disruption linked to psychiatric conditions

## Development and Aging

### Development
- DMN connectivity strengthens during childhood/adolescence
- Local to distributed organization shift
- Long-range connections develop later

### Aging
- DMN connectivity decreases with age
- PCC-mPFC correlation weakens
- Correlates with cognitive decline
- Potential early dementia marker

## Clinical Relevance

### Alzheimer's Disease
- PCC hypometabolism is early marker
- DMN connectivity disrupted
- Amyloid deposition in DMN regions

### Psychiatric Conditions
- **Schizophrenia**: Reduced DMN suppression during tasks
- **Depression**: Altered DMN connectivity
- **ADHD**: Reduced DMN deactivation

## Role in Whole-Brain Modeling

### Modeling Considerations
- DMN shows distinct dynamics from task networks
- Anticorrelation must be captured
- Resting-state fluctuations important
- Hub organization affects signal spread

### Applications
- Understanding resting-state activity
- Modeling cognitive control
- Studying psychiatric conditions
- Predicting cognitive decline

# A Default Mode of Brain Function

**Authors:** Marcus E. Raichle, Abraham Z. Snyder, et al.  
**Year:** 2001  
**Venue:** PNAS

## Key Contributions

- Introduced the concept of "default mode" brain function
- Identified brain regions active during rest and deactivated during tasks
- Proposed functional significance of intrinsic brain activity
- Established foundation for resting-state fMRI research

## Abstract Summary

The authors observed that specific brain regions are consistently active when the brain is at rest and deactivate during goal-directed tasks. They termed this the "default mode of brain function" and proposed it represents a baseline state of organized, intrinsic activity.

## Significance

Seminal paper that launched the field of resting-state functional connectivity and the study of the default mode network. One of the most cited papers in neuroscience.

# The Brain's Default Network: Anatomy, Function, and Relevance to Disease

**Authors:** Randy L. Buckner, Jessica R. Andrews-Hanna, Daniel L. Schacter  
**Year:** 2008  
**Venue:** Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences

## Key Contributions

- Comprehensive anatomical description of the default mode network
- Integration of function and anatomy of DMN regions
- Discussion of DMN role in self-referential processing and memory
- Review of DMN alterations in neurological and psychiatric disease

## Abstract Summary

This review provides a detailed anatomical and functional characterization of the default mode network. The authors discuss the core DMN regions (medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex, angular gyrus, hippocampus), their anatomical connections, and their role in internally-directed cognition.

## Significance

Foundational review establishing the anatomical basis of the DMN and its functional significance, essential reference for DMN research.

# Functional Connectivity in the Resting Brain

**Authors:** Michael D. Greicius, Ben Krasnow, Allan L. Reiss, Vinod Menon  
**Year:** 2003  
**Venue:** PNAS

## Key Contributions

- First demonstration of functional connectivity within DMN using resting-state fMRI
- Showed correlation between posterior cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex
- Established seed-based correlation analysis for resting-state data
- Validated DMN as a functionally connected network

## Abstract Summary

Using resting-state fMRI, the authors demonstrated that brain regions identified as the default mode network show strong temporal correlations in spontaneous activity. Seed-based correlation analysis revealed functional connectivity between posterior cingulate cortex, medial prefrontal cortex, and lateral parietal cortex.

## Significance

Landmark paper establishing resting-state functional connectivity as a method for studying large-scale brain networks, validating the DMN as a coherent functional system.
