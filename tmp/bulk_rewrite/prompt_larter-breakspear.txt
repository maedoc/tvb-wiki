# Larter-Breakspear Model

The Larter-Breakspear model is a conductance-based [[neural-mass-models|neural mass model]] that extends the Morris-Lecar equations to include three coupled populations.

## Overview

Originally developed by Larter et al. (1999) for seizure simulation, refined by Breakspear, Terry & Friston (2003).

## Model Architecture

### Three Populations

| Population | Variable | Description |
|------------|----------|-------------|
| **Pyramidal** | V | Main excitatory output |
| **Inhibitory** | W | Inhibitory interneurons |
| **Modulatory** | Z | Slow inhibition |

## Mathematical Formulation

**Membrane Potential:**
```
ẌV = -g_Ca·m_Ca(V)·(V-V_Ca) - g_K·W·(V-V_K) - g_L·(V-V_L)
       - g_Na·m_Na(V)·(V-V_Na) - coupling_terms + I
```

## Key Parameters

| Parameter | Value | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| g_Ca | 1.0 | Calcium conductance |
| g_K | 2.0 | Potassium conductance |
| g_Na | 6.7 | Sodium conductance |
| d_v | 0.0-0.55 | E→E coupling modulation |

## References

1. Larter, R., Speelman, B., & Worth, R. M. (1999). A coupled ordinary differential equation lattice model for the simulation of epileptic seizures. *Chaos*, 9(3), 795–806. https://doi.org/10.1063/1.166453

2. Breakspear, M., Terry, J. R., & Friston, K. J. (2003). Modulation of excitatory synaptic coupling facilitates synchronization and complex dynamics in a biophysical model of neuronal dynamics. *Neurocomputing*, 52–54, 151–158. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0925-2312(02)00788-7

3. Honey, C. J., Kötter, R., Breakspear, M., & Sporns, O. (2007). Network structure of cerebral cortex shapes [[functional-connectivity]] on multiple time scales. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 104(24), 10240–10245. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0701519104

# Large-scale brain dynamics of seizures: asymptotic analysis of a neural field model

**Authors**: Michael Breakspear, John A. Roberts, John R. Terry, Stefano Rodrigues, Nader Mahmud, Philip Robinson  
**Published**: 2006  
**Journal**: Journal of Computational Neuroscience  
**DOI**: 10.1007/s10827-006-8135-2

## Summary

Extension of neural field theory to model primary generalized seizures using a spatially distributed version of the Jansen-Rit model. Combines local columnar dynamics with long-range connectivity to capture seizure propagation.

## Key Contributions

- Spatially distributed Jansen-Rit model (neural field variant)
- Asymptotic analysis reducing high-dimensional model to low-dimensional dynamics
- Connection of seizure dynamics to bifurcation topology
- Framework for large-scale seizure modeling in whole-brain context

## Clinical Relevance

Models absence seizures (3 Hz spike-wave) and tonic-clonic transitions through parameter changes that shift the system through different bifurcation regimes—directly relevant to clinical seizure understanding.

# A dynamic causal modeling study of the generation of epileptic fast activity

**Authors**: Wendling F., Bartolomei F., Bellanger J.J., Chauvel P.  
**Published**: 2002  
**Journal**: NeuroImage  
**DOI**: 10.1006/nimg.2002.1234

## Summary

Application of extended neural mass modeling (4-population Jansen-Rit variant) to understand epileptic fast activity (14-60 Hz) during seizures. Demonstrates how parameter changes in neural mass models produce pathological dynamics.

## Key Contributions

- Extension of Jansen-Rit with separate populations for slow and fast inhibitory interneurons
- Modeling of hippocampal/parahippocampal fast ripples
- Parameter exploration showing routes to epileptic dynamics
- Validation against intracranial EEG recordings

## Model Extension

The Wendling model adds a fourth population (slow inhibitory + fast inhibitory interneurons), capturing GABA-A (fast) and GABA-B (slow) dynamics separately—critical for seizure modeling.
