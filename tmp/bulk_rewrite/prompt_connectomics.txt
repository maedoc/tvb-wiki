# Connectomics

The field of neuroscience concerned with mapping and analyzing the brain's connections at various scales.

## Definition

Connectomics is the study of the [[connectome]] – the complete set of neural connections in the brain. It encompasses both the empirical mapping of brain connectivity and the theoretical analysis of connectivity patterns using network science.

## Historical Development

### Origins (2005)
The term was introduced by olaf sporns|Sporns, giulio tononi|Tononi, and rolf kotter|Kötter in their seminal paper establishing connectomics as a field.

### Major Initiatives
- **2010s**: human-connectome-project|[[human-connectome-project]] launched
- **2016**: uk-biobank|[[uk-biobank]] Imaging begins
- **Ongoing**: Multiple species connectome projects

## Approaches

### Structural Connectomics
- **[[diffusion-mri]]**: White matter [[tractography]]
- **Tracing Studies**: Histological methods in animals
- **Electron Microscopy**: Synaptic-level connectivity

### Functional Connectomics
- **[[resting-state|Resting-State fMRI]]**: Spontaneous activity correlations
- **Task-Based [[fmri]]**: Evoked connectivity patterns
- **EEG/MEG**: Electrophysiological connectivity

## Analysis Methods

### Network Science
- graph-theory|[[network-dynamics|Graph theory]]: Mathematical framework
- small-world-networks|Small-world analysis
- modularity|[[modularity]] detection
- rich-club|[[rich-club|Rich-club organization]]

### Computational Tools
- brain-connectivity-toolbox|[[brain-connectivity-toolbox]]
- Network visualization software
- Connectome databases

## Applications

### Basic Neuroscience
- Understanding brain organization
- Structure-function relationships
- Development and [[aging]]

### Clinical
- Neurological disorders
- Psychiatric conditions
- Biomarker discovery

# The Human Connectome: A Structural Description of the Human Brain

**Authors:** Olaf Sporns, Giulio Tononi, Rolf Kötter  
**Year:** 2005  
**Venue:** PLoS Computational Biology

## Key Contributions

- Introduced the term "connectome" as a comprehensive structural description of the human brain
- Proposed that understanding connectivity is essential for understanding brain function
- Outlined technical requirements for mapping the human connectome
- Emphasized the importance of network analysis approaches

## Abstract Summary

The authors argue that understanding the structural organization of the brain is crucial for understanding its function. They propose the concept of the "connectome" - a comprehensive structural description of the network of elements and connections forming the human brain. The paper discusses the theoretical importance of the connectome and outlines potential approaches for mapping it.

## Significance

This is the seminal paper that introduced the term "connectome" to neuroscience, sparking the field of connectomics. The concept has since become central to modern neuroscience, driving major initiatives like the Human Connectome Project.
