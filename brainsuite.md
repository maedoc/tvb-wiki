BrainSuite is a collection of open-source software tools developed at the UCLA Brain Mapping Center for processing and analyzing magnetic resonance images (MRI) of the human brain. The primary purpose of BrainSuite is to extract and parameterize the inner and outer surfaces of the cerebral cortex, segment and label gray and white matter structures, analyze diffusion imaging data, and process functional MRI. Originally developed by David W. Shattuck and Richard M. Leahy, the software has evolved into a comprehensive neuroimaging toolkit used primarily for cortical surface reconstruction, parcellation, and regional analysis. The current stable release is BrainSuite23a, which includes the USCBrain atlas, improved cortical thickness estimation, and a fully containerized BIDS App workflow.

## Key Features

BrainSuite provides a complete processing pipeline for T1-weighted structural MRI that transforms raw volumetric images into anatomically labeled cortical surface models. The pipeline consists of multiple stages that can be run interactively through the graphical user interface or executed in batch mode via command-line scripts. Each stage produces intermediate outputs that can be inspected and selectively re-processed, which is particularly valuable when debugging challenging acquisitions or optimizing parameters for specific populations.

The first major processing stage is skull stripping, which uses the Brain Surface Extractor (BSE) algorithm combining anisotropic diffusion filtering, Marr-Hildreth edge detection, and mathematical morphology operators to remove non-brain tissue from the MRI volume. Following skull stripping, the Bias Field Correction (BFC) module estimates and corrects for shading artifacts caused by radiofrequency field inhomogeneities by fitting a partial volume measurement model to local regions of interest and interpolating the correction field using tri-cubic B-splines.

Tissue classification follows, assigning each voxel to white matter, gray matter, or cerebrospinal fluid classes using a spatial prior that models the contiguous nature of brain tissue types. This classification step produces both discrete labels and fractional partial volume estimates, which are crucial for accurately capturing tissue boundaries in the cortex where partial volume effects are pronounced due to the thin cortical ribbon.

Cortical surface extraction proceeds through topology correction using a graph-based algorithm that ensures the inner cortical boundary is topologically equivalent to a sphere (genus zero), which is essential for subsequent surface-based analyses and group-wise registration. The wisp filter then removes small misclassified voxel clusters that would otherwise create artifactual bumps or strands on the surface. Finally, surface meshes are generated for both the inner (white matter) and pial (outer cortical) boundaries using isocontour methods, with the pial surface generated by iteratively moving vertices outward until they encounter cerebrospinal fluid or self-intersection would occur.

Cortical thickness estimation in recent BrainSuite versions uses the Anisotropic Laplace Equation (ALE) method, which provides more accurate thickness measurements than the previous linked-distance approach by solving the Laplace equation with boundary conditions at both the inner and outer cortical surfaces. The thicknessPVC module implements this method and produces voxel-wise thickness maps that can be mapped to atlas space for group analyses.

Surface-constrained Volumetric Registration (SVReg) aligns subject MRI to a labeled atlas using both volumetric and surface-based information, enabling automatic labeling of anatomical regions of interest. The USCBrain atlas distributed with BrainSuite provides 130 cortical regions and 29 noncortical regions, with sub-gyral parcellations derived from resting-state functional connectivity data from the Human Connectome Project. This hybrid anatomical-functional approach produces more anatomically meaningful parcellations than purely anatomical atlases, as functionally coherent subregions are delineated within each gyral region.

BrainSuite also includes a diffusion MRI processing pipeline (BDP) that performs geometric distortion correction using the T1-weighted structural as an anatomical reference, fits diffusion tensors and orientation distribution functions, and supports tractography. The BrainSuite BIDS App provides containerized workflows following the BIDS standard, enabling reproducible analyses across different computing environments.

## Relationship to TVB

BrainSuite is tangentially related to [[the-virtual-brain]] in the context of whole-brain modeling workflows, though the two tools serve distinct purposes in the neuroimaging processing chain. BrainSuite produces anatomical data—cortical surfaces, thickness maps, and parcellated region labels—while TVB uses such structural MRI data as inputs for constructing personalized brain models. In practice, the structural [[connectivity]] matrices derived from [[diffusion-imaging]] and [[tractography]] processing can be imported into TVB to define the white matter pathways connecting brain regions in whole-brain simulations.

The cortical parcellations produced by BrainSuite (particularly the USCBrain atlas with its 130 regional labels) can serve as regional definitions for TVB connectomes, providing a finer-grained parcellation than coarser atlases like AAL. Additionally, cortical thickness estimates from BrainSuite could theoretically inform parameters related to regional cortical volume or surface area in TVB models, though such integration is not currently part of standard TVB workflows. For users seeking to combine these tools, BrainSuite outputs in NIfTI format can be converted to TVB-compatible formats using standard neuroimaging libraries like [[nibabel]].

The most direct connection between BrainSuite and TVB lies in their shared reliance on high-quality structural MRI processing: BrainSuite provides the anatomical segmentation and parcellation foundation that enables construction of detailed [[structural-connectivity]] matrices, while TVB consumes these matrices to simulate large-scale brain dynamics. Both tools benefit from advances in [[neuroimaging]] preprocessing and can be integrated in personalized brain modeling pipelines.

## Key Papers

The foundational BrainSuite algorithm for cortical surface extraction was described in Shattuck and Leahy (2001), which introduced the graph-based topology correction approach that remains central to the software. The USCBrain atlas methodology was published in Joshi et al. (2022) in the Journal of Neuroscience Methods, describing the hybrid anatomical-functional parcellation approach using resting-state fMRI data from the Human Connectome Project. The BrainSuite BIDS App is described in a preprint by Kim et al. (2023), and the anisotropic Laplace equation method for cortical thickness estimation was introduced in Joshi et al. (2018).

## Related Software

BrainSuite occupies a similar niche in the neuroimaging ecosystem as other cortical surface extraction tools such as [[afni]] and [[fsl]]. For visualization and further analysis of BrainSuite outputs, the [[connectome-workbench]] tool is commonly used to display cortical surfaces and statistical maps. The BrainSuite BIDS App is designed to integrate with [[bids]]-compatible preprocessing pipelines including [[fmriprep]] for functional MRI processing. For diffusion processing specifically, alternative tools include MRtrix3, [[dsi-studio]], and [[camino]].
