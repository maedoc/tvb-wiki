Brain-Score is an open-source benchmarking platform designed to systematically evaluate computational brain models against empirical neural and behavioral data. The platform establishes standardized scoring functions that enable objective comparison of diverse modeling approaches—including [[neural-mass-models]], [[spiking-neural-networks]], and [[whole-brain-modeling]] frameworks—based on their ability to reproduce observed brain activity patterns. By providing a common evaluation framework, Brain-Score addresses the comparability challenge that arises when equations, parameters, networks, and numerical settings are reported inconsistently across studies [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]], and when computational work remains confined to isolated spatial and temporal scales [[raw/papers/arxiv-2505.16861.md|Hater et al. (2025)]][[raw/papers/semanticscholar-eb704b6f5462.md|Hater et al. (2026)]]. This fragmentation makes it difficult to determine which architectural choices actually improve biological fidelity, a gap that Brain-Score targets through quantitative scoring against empirical recordings.

## Motivation and Context

The reproducibility crisis in computational neuroscience stems from inconsistent reporting of equations, parameters, networks, and numerical settings across studies [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]]. Shared simulation code is often not fully documented, standardized, or executable, which fragments the modeling landscape and impedes cross-study comparison [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]]. This fragmentation is compounded by the field's traditional focus on isolated spatial and temporal scales, limiting understanding of brain function across multiple levels [[raw/papers/arxiv-2505.16861.md|Hater et al. (2025)]][[raw/papers/semanticscholar-eb704b6f5462.md|Hater et al. (2026)]]. Benchmarking platforms such as Brain-Score aim to mitigate these challenges by applying standardized evaluation principles to neuroscience, replacing informal group-specific metrics with quantitative scoring against empirical [[neuroimaging-fmri]], [[neuroimaging-eeg]], and [[neuroimaging-meg]] recordings.

The need for rigorous comparability has motivated parallel efforts to develop semantic knowledge bases and minimal metadata standards that standardize model descriptions and enable cross-platform code generation [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]]. Brain-Score operates within this landscape by focusing specifically on quantitative validation through scoring model predictions against ground-truth neural and behavioral data. This empirical scoring layer complements metadata-driven reproducibility initiatives by offering objective metrics for model comparison [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]]. The platform aggregates performance across multiple benchmarks into a single scalar summary alongside detailed per-task breakdowns, offering a level of transparency that aligns with broader [[reproducibility]] goals in [[machine-learning]] and computational neuroscience.

## Technical Framework

Brain-Score organizes benchmarks into three hierarchical tiers. Neural benchmarks evaluate whether a model's internal representations match recorded neural activity in specific brain regions. Behavioral benchmarks assess whether model outputs predict observed responses such as reaction times or choice probabilities. Cognitive architecture benchmarks probe higher-level functions such as working memory or attention. Each benchmark pairs stimuli with empirical data and applies a scoring function to quantify the similarity between predictions and ground-truth recordings. This tiered structure mirrors the multi-scale integration challenge that arises when coupling detailed spiking networks to population-level neural mass dynamics in co-simulation frameworks [[raw/papers/arxiv-2505.16861.md|Hater et al. (2025)]][[raw/papers/semanticscholar-eb704b6f5462.md|Hater et al. (2026)]]. By spanning neural, behavioral, and cognitive levels, Brain-Score provides a validation architecture that can accommodate the diverse [[network-dynamics]] and [[connectome]] structures generated by modern simulators, supporting the cross-platform portability that standardized metadata standards are designed to enable [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]].

## Relationship to TVB

Brain-Score represents a complementary validation paradigm to whole-brain modeling frameworks such as [[tvb|The Virtual Brain]]. TVB excels at simulating large-scale [[network-dynamics]] at the regional level, integrating [[structural-connectivity]] into [[neural-mass-models]] to generate [[functional-connectivity]] patterns [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. Brain-Score focuses on finer-grained validation against empirical data, which could potentially assess how well TVB's neural mass implementations reproduce observed dynamics at specific regional scales. Conversely, large-scale Brain-Score benchmarks that probe network-level dynamics could leverage TVB's sophisticated structural connectivity pipelines and [[parameter-estimation]] methods [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. The two approaches address different scales of the validation problem, with Brain-Score providing targeted benchmarks and TVB providing the simulation infrastructure for exploring how components interact in whole-brain networks. Multi-scale co-simulation frameworks such as Arbor-TVB demonstrate the technical feasibility of coupling detailed spiking networks to whole-brain neural mass dynamics, a challenge that any benchmarking platform spanning multiple scales must ultimately accommodate [[raw/papers/arxiv-2505.16861.md|Hater et al. (2025)]][[raw/papers/semanticscholar-eb704b6f5462.md|Hater et al. (2026)]]. The TVB Ontology project further establishes a foundation for transparent model sharing and cross-platform comparability by generating executable code for multiple simulators and exporting FAIR metadata, creating an ecosystem in which benchmarking platforms can operate rigorously [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-9afbfd2d37be.md|Martin et al. (2025)]].
