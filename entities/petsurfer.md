PETSurfer is a specialized software suite embedded within the [[freesurfer|FreeSurfer]] framework that provides end-to-end analysis capabilities for positron emission tomography (PET) [[neuroimaging]] data. It integrates PET image processing with the anatomical segmentations and cortical surface reconstructions generated by FreeSurfer, enabling researchers to perform motion correction, PET-to-MRI registration, partial volume correction (PVC), reference region kinetic modeling, and group analyses across region-of-interest (ROI), volume, and surface-based spaces. Originally developed at the Martinos Center for Biomedical Imaging at Massachusetts General Hospital, PETSurfer addresses a critical need in quantitative PET neuroimaging: the systematic correction of partial volume effects that arise from the limited spatial resolution of PET scanners, which cause signal from different tissue types to bleed into single voxels.

The motivation for developing PETSurfer stems from fundamental limitations in PET quantification that affect both clinical and research applications. PET images suffer from relatively poor spatial resolution compared to magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), typically ranging from 4-8 mm full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) depending on the scanner and reconstruction method. This blurring means that any given voxel in a PET image contains signals from a mixture of tissue types—gray matter, [[white-matter]], and cerebrospinal fluid—making it difficult to attribute measured radioactivity concentrations to specific neuroanatomical structures. Without appropriate correction, partial volume effects lead to systematic underestimation of radiotracer uptake in small structures and混淆 group comparisons, particularly in studies of neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer's disease where atrophy is a key confounder. The importance of proper PVC for accurate PET quantification in conditions such as Alzheimer's disease has been extensively documented, with different correction methods potentially leading to substantially different conclusions about disease-related changes in cerebral glucose metabolism.

PETSurfer provides multiple PVC methods that can be selected based on the analysis approach. For ROI-based analysis, the Symmetric Geometric Transfer Matrix (SGTM) method is used, which models the contribution of each tissue type to each ROI based on the known point spread function (PSF) of the scanner and the tissue fraction within each region. For voxel-wise analysis, PETSurfer implements the two-compartment model (also known as the Meltzer method), the three-compartment model (the Müller-Gärtner method), and the region-based voxel-wise (RBV) approach. All PVC implementations account for the tissue fraction effect (TFE), which separately corrects for the mixture of tissue types within each voxel before applying the PSF correction. The choice of PVC method can significantly impact quantitative results, and the literature has shown that different methods may lead to different conclusions in applications such as [[aging]] studies, where age-related atrophy can interact with partial volume effects to produce spurious findings.

In addition to PVC, PETSurfer provides integrated support for reference region kinetic modeling approaches, including MRTM1 (reference tissue model 1), MRTM2 (reference tissue model 2), and Logan graphical analysis. These methods allow for the estimation of binding potential without requiring arterial blood sampling, by using a reference region with known negligible specific binding. The kinetic modeling capabilities are seamlessly integrated with FreeSurfer-defined ROIs, making it straightforward to extract time-activity curves from anatomically defined regions. This integration extends to surface-based analysis, where PET data can be sampled onto the cortical surface for visualization and statistical analysis using the same general [[linear|linear model]] (GLM) framework that FreeSurfer provides for [[neuroimaging-fmri|functional MRI]] analysis.

A distinctive feature of PETSurfer is its "three-space approach" for voxel-wise analysis of cortical regions. This approach analyzes the cortex and subcortical structures in separate processing streams: cortical gray matter is analyzed on the left and right hemisphere surfaces (making three spaces in total: left cortex, right cortex, and subcortical gray matter). Surface-based analysis offers several advantages over volumetric approaches for cortical data, including the ability to perform 2D smoothing along the cortical ribbon, which avoids the exacerbation of partial volume effects that occurs when 3D volumetric smoothing is applied across cortical boundaries. This method has been shown to reduce both bias and variance in kinetic modeling of brain PET data compared to purely volumetric approaches. The three spaces can be recombined into a more traditional volume space for visualization and comparison with subcortical results.

The workflow in PETSurfer typically begins with a standard FreeSurfer reconstruction of the subject's T1-weighted structural MRI (using `recon-all`), followed by generation of a high-resolution segmentation for GTM analysis using the `gtmseg` command. This segmentation combines cortical parcellations from the Desikan-Killiany atlas (or custom atlases) with subcortical segmentations from the automatic segmentation suite, creating a unified anatomical framework for PET analysis. PET images are then coregistered to the anatomical MRI using rigid-body registration, and PVC and kinetic modeling are applied using the `mri_gtmpvc` and `mri_glmfit` commands respectively. The integration with FreeSurfer also enables customization of the segmentation using alternative cortical atlases or custom ROIs, making PETSurfer adaptable to diverse research questions.

The relationship between PETSurfer and [[the-virtual-brain|The Virtual Brain]] (TVB) lies primarily in the domain of multimodal neuroimaging integration for [[whole-brain|whole-brain modeling]]. TVB supports the incorporation of empirical PET data as an empirical constraint for model fitting, particularly in applications involving [[metabolic-modeling]] and the simulation of [[brain-dynamics]] in neurodegenerative diseases. When TVB users process PET data for connectivity analysis or model [[parameter-estimation]], PVC using methods like those implemented in PETSurfer becomes relevant for ensuring that the empirical PET signals accurately reflect regional cerebral metabolic rates or receptor binding potentials without contamination from partial volume effects. Additionally, TVB's integration with [[freesurfer|FreeSurfer]] for [[structural-connectivity]] derivation creates a natural pathway for incorporating PETSurfer-processed PET metrics as supplementary empirical data in hybrid modeling approaches. The cortical surface-based analysis capabilities of PETSurfer also complement TVB's surface-based simulation frameworks, enabling consistent representation of cortical dynamics across imaging modalities.

## Key Commands

| Command | Function |
|---|---|
| `gtmseg` | Create high-resolution segmentation for GTM-based PVC |
| `mri_coreg` | Register PET image to FreeSurfer anatomical |
| `mri_gtmpvc` | Apply PVC with GTM, MG, RBV methods; extract ROI TACs |
| `mri_vol2surf` | Sample PET data onto cortical surface |
| `mri_glmfit` | Run kinetic modeling (MRTM1, MRTM2, Logan) and group GLM |
