PETsurfer is a software toolkit for processing and analyzing Positron Emission Tomography (PET) [[neuroimaging]] data within the FreeSurfer ecosystem. It provides a standardized pipeline for preprocessing PET images, performing partial volume correction, and conducting voxelwise and region-of-interest analyses that leverage the anatomical parcellations generated by FreeSurfer's structural MRI segmentation.

## Overview

PETsurfer integrates PET image analysis directly into the [[FreeSurfer]] workflow, enabling researchers to combine the high-resolution cortical and subcortical segmentation from structural MRI with PET ligand binding measurements. This integration is particularly valuable for studying neurodegenerative diseases where both structural brain changes and molecular pathologies—such as amyloid plaque accumulation in Alzheimer's disease or dopaminergic deficits in Parkinson's disease—are of interest. The software handles the core preprocessing steps including motion correction, spatial normalization to the FreeSurfer anatomical space, and registration between PET and MRI volumes[^Greve2014]. By performing these analyses in the native FreeSurfer space, PETsurfer enables direct comparison of PET findings with cortical thickness measurements, volumetric measures, and other FreeSurfer-derived metrics without requiring additional registration steps between different image spaces.

## Key Features

The toolkit offers several specialized capabilities for PET analysis. Partial volume correction is one of its most important features, addressing the spatial blurring inherent in PET imaging that can cause signal leakage between adjacent brain regions. PETsurfer implements multiple partial volume correction algorithms including the Müller-Gärtner method[^Muller1996] and the regional voxel-wise (RBV) method[^Thomas2009], allowing researchers to choose the method most appropriate for their research question. The software supports both region-of-interest analyses using FreeSurfer's atlases (such as the Desikan-Killiany atlas and [[destrieux-atlas]]) and voxelwise cortical surface-based analyses, providing flexibility for different analytical approaches.

PETsurfer supports multiple PET tracer protocols including fluorine-18 fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) for glucose metabolism, amyloid-binding tracers like florbetapir and flutemetamol for beta-amyloid detection, and tau-binding radiotracers for studying neurofibrillary pathology. The output includes both graphical summaries and statistical maps that can be visualized using FreeSurfer's [[freeview]] tool or exported for use in other statistical packages.

## Relationship to TVB

While PETsurfer is primarily designed for PET neuroimaging analysis and not specifically developed for [[whole-brain|whole-brain modeling]], it has indirect relevance to [[TVB]] workflows in several ways. First, PET-derived metrics such as glucose metabolism (FDG-PET) and amyloid burden can serve as subject-specific constraints for [[personalized-brain-modeling|personalized brain]] models in TVB, providing empirical data on regional neural activity or pathology that can inform model parameterization. Second, the structural parcellations generated by FreeSurfer and used by PETsurfer are commonly used as the basis for TVB's [[brain-parcellations]], establishing methodological compatibility between the two toolkits. Third, researchers studying [[epilepsy-modeling]] or Alzheimer's disease using TVB may incorporate PET imaging data processed through PETsurfer to validate their simulations against empirical functional and molecular imaging data.

## Related Software

PETsurfer is closely integrated with the [[FreeSurfer]] software suite, particularly relying on the anatomical segmentations and surface reconstructions it produces. For PET preprocessing, it works with various DICOM converters and image formatting tools including dcm2niix. Visualization of PETsurfer results typically employs freeview for viewing overlays on cortical surfaces. Researchers comparing PET findings with [[functional-connectivity]] analyses may also work with tools like conn for functional [[connectivity]] analysis, nilearn for neuroimage processing in Python, and the [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]] for graph-theoretic analysis of brain networks derived from PET-informed parcellations.
