PETSurfer is a software package that extends the FreeSurfer [[neuroimaging]] toolkit to enable processing and analysis of positron emission tomography (PET) brain imaging data. Developed as a module within the FreeSurfer framework, PETSurfer integrates PET quantification capabilities directly with the cortical and subcortical segmentation workflows that FreeSurfer provides for magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). This integration allows researchers to perform region-of-interest (ROI)-based PET analysis using the same anatomical parcellations derived from structural MRI, ensuring spatial correspondence between anatomical segmentation and PET uptake measurements.

## Motivation and Context

The analysis of PET neuroimaging data presents unique challenges that differ from other modalities. PET images have lower spatial resolution compared to MRI, and the signal reflects radiotracer uptake rather than anatomical structure. Early approaches to PET quantification required manual or semi-automated region definition, introducing user variability and limiting [[reproducibility]] across studies. Additionally, partial volume effects—the blurring of signal across tissue boundaries due to limited spatial resolution—can significantly bias quantitative measurements, particularly in small brain structures.

PETSurfer was developed to address these challenges by leveraging the precise anatomical segmentations provided by FreeSurfer. By applying these MRI-derived parcellations to PET data, researchers can obtain consistent ROI-based measurements that align with the established FreeSurfer pipeline used throughout the neuroimaging community. This approach is particularly valuable in studies of [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer's disease]] and related dementias, where amyloid and tau PET imaging biomarkers require reliable quantification across large cohort studies and multi-site collaborations.

## Technical Implementation

PETSurfer operates by mapping PET data onto the anatomical surfaces and volumes generated by the standard FreeSurfer processing pipeline. The workflow begins with the standard FreeSurfer reconstruction of T1-weighted MRI scans, which produces cortical ribbon meshes, subcortical segmentation, and atlas-based parcellations such as the Desikan-Killiany atlas and the [[schaefer]] atlases. These anatomical segmentations serve as templates for PET quantification.

The core technical challenge PETSurfer addresses is the partial volume correction. Because PET spatial resolution is typically 4-5 mm full-width at half-maximum (FWHM), signal from small structures is contaminated by neighboring tissues. PETSurfer implements model-based partial volume correction algorithms that deconvolve the PET point spread function from the measured signal, allowing more accurate estimation of true radiotracer uptake in small cortical and subcortical regions. This is particularly important for studies examining regional tau accumulation in Alzheimer's disease, where patterns of pathology follow specific anatomical distributions.

PETSurfer supports multiple PET radiotracers commonly used in neuroimaging research, including amyloid tracers such as Pittsburgh compound B (PiB) and florbetapir, tau tracers such as flortaucipir (AV-45), andFDG (fluorodeoxyglucose) for measuring cerebral metabolic rate of glucose. The software outputs standardized uptake value ratios (SUVR) using reference regions such as cerebellar gray matter or the whole cerebellum, enabling cross-subject and cross-cohort comparisons.

## Integration with Whole-Brain Modeling

In the context of [[whole-brain|whole-brain modeling]] and [[computational-neuroscience]], PETSurfer serves as a critical bridge between empirical neuroimaging data and [[personalized-brain-modeling|personalized brain]] models. The regional time series extracted using PETSurfer parcellations can inform [[functional-connectivity]] analyses in pathological populations, particularly where PET-based markers provide complementary information to [[fmri]] blood-oxygen-level-dependent signals.

For [[the-virtual-brain]] (TVB), PETSurfer-derived regional measurements may be used to constrain personalized brain models in several ways. [[structural-connectivity]] matrices derived from [[diffusion-imaging]] can be weighted or modulated using PET-derived regional activity or pathology burden estimates. Inepilepsy-modeling applications, PETSurfer-generated hypometabolic patterns from FDG-PET can inform seizure onset zone localization used in TVB simulations. The integration of PET biomarkers with TVB's multi-scale framework enables more biologically-grounded simulations of [[brain-dynamics]] in disease states.

## Relationship to Other Tools

PETSurfer builds directly on the [[freesurfer]] infrastructure and shares methodological roots with other FreeSurfer extensions. Unlike general-purpose PET analysis tools that require separate anatomical templates, PETSurfer leverages the same FreeSurfer outputs used by [[fmri]] preprocessing pipelines, enabling seamless multi-modal integration. This contrasts with tools like [[spm]] or [[fsl]], which implement independent processing streams for PET data.

Compared to commercial PET analysis solutions such as Siemens NeuroQ or GE Advantage Workstation, PETSurfer provides open-source accessibility and integration with the broader FreeSurfer ecosystem. The recent development of clinical tools like Heuron Brain PET demonstrates the growing interest in automated PET quantification, though PETSurfer remains primarily used in research settings due to its computational requirements and dependency on FreeSurfer processing.

For cortical thickness and volumetric analysis, PETSurfer complements [[freesurfer]] by providing the molecular imaging layer atop the structural measurements already available. Studies comparing different FreeSurfer versions have demonstrated the importance of consistent processing pipelines for longitudinal PET analysis, where changes in segmentation algorithms can introduce confounds in longitudinal PET metrics.

## Key Features and Applications

The primary applications of PETSurfer include amyloid and tau PET quantification in Alzheimer's disease research, FDG-PET analysis for metabolic mapping in epilepsy and dementia, and multi-tracer PET studies examining the relationship between different molecular pathologies. The software enables both region-of-interest analysis and vertex-wise surface-based analysis, allowing researchers to examine cortical patterns of radiotracer uptake at fine spatial scales.

PETSurfer's integration with the broader neuroimaging ecosystem extends through its compatibility with data processed using tools like [[fsl]], [[afni]], and [[spm]]. The outputs can be visualized using [[freesurfer]]'s freeview or exported to standard formats for third-party visualization packages. For researchers working with large cohort studies such as the [[hcp-dataset]], PETSurfer provides a documented pipeline for consistent PET analysis across participants with available MRI data.
