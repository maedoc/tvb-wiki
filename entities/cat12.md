# CAT12

## Overview

CAT12 (Computational Anatomy Toolbox 12) is a MATLAB-based toolbox for computational anatomy and voxel-based morphometry (VBM) analysis of brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) data. Developed and maintained by the Structural Brain Mapping Group at the University of Jena, Germany, under the direction of Christian Gaser, CAT12 provides a comprehensive suite of tools for preprocessing, analyzing, and quantifying structural brain anatomy from T1-weighted MRI scans. The toolbox has become a standard in the neuroimaging community for extracting morphometric measures that characterize individual differences in brain structure, making it particularly valuable for studies of neurodevelopment, aging, and neurological disorders.

## Key Features

CAT12 offers several integrated functionalities that distinguish it from other morphometric analysis tools. The core pipeline implements a modified version of the "New Segment" algorithm originally developed for [[spm]], incorporating enhanced tissue segmentation that better accounts for partial volume effects and produces more accurate gray matter (GM), white matter (WM), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) probability maps [[#ref-1]]([[#ref-1]]). The toolbox employs Dartel (Diffeomorphic Anatomical Registration Through Exponentiated Lie Algebra) as its registration engine, which creates a flow field that aligns participants' anatomical data to a study-specific template through a series of increasingly fine deformations [[#ref-2]]([[#ref-2]]).

Surface processing in CAT12 represents a major strength, enabling reconstruction of the inner and outer cortical surfaces from the segmented brain masks. From these surfaces, the toolbox computes cortical thickness measurements using the Euclidean distance between corresponding vertices on the inner and outer surfaces—a method that complements the volume-based thickness estimates derived from VBM [[#ref-3]]([[#ref-3]]). Additionally, CAT12 provides automated parcellation of cortical and subcortical regions using established atlases, allowing researchers to extract region-of-interest (ROI) statistics efficiently.

The preprocessing pipeline in CAT12 includes several configurable preprocessing options: bias field correction to account for signal inhomogeneities, skull-stripping for brain extraction, and affine registration to Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space. The toolbox implements a sophisticated quality control (QC) system that generates visual reports and produces automated quality ratings, helping researchers identify problematic scans before downstream analysis [[#ref-4]]([[#ref-4]]). Recent versions of CAT12 also support longitudinal analysis for tracking morphological changes over time within individuals.

## Relationship to TVB

CAT12 plays an important role in [[the-virtual-brain]] workflows for generating personalized brain models. The structural MRI preprocessing capabilities of CAT12 produce the gray matter volume and cortical thickness maps that can be integrated into TVB's pipeline for constructing individual-specific brain parcellations. When combined with diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) data processed through tools like [[mrtrix3]] or [[freesurfer]], the morphometric outputs from CAT12 contribute to the structural connectivity matrices that form the anatomical backbone of whole-brain simulations.

In practice, researchers using TVB for personalized brain modeling will often employ CAT12 to process T1-weighted scans, extracting cortical thickness and regional volume measures that inform the neural mass model parameters at each brain region. The parcellations generated by CAT12 can be directly imported into TVB's interface, enabling researchers to define the cortical and subcortical regions that constitute the nodes of the whole-brain network model. This integration exemplifies the broader relationship between morphometric analysis tools like CAT12 and large-scale brain simulators, where detailed anatomical information serves as the foundation for dynamical models of brain function.

## Key Papers

The foundational method of voxel-based morphometry was introduced by Ashburner and Friston [[#ref-5]]([[#ref-5]]), establishing the statistical framework for detecting regional differences in brain tissue concentration that CAT12 builds upon. The original Dartel registration algorithm, which forms the core of CAT12's spatial normalization pipeline, was described by Ashburner [[#ref-2]]([[#ref-2]]). CAT12's enhanced segmentation and surface-based thickness estimation methods are documented in technical reports accompanying each release [[#ref-1]]([[#ref-1]]), with the cortical thickness approach rigorously validated by Dahnke et al. [[#ref-3]]([[#ref-3]]). The Structural Brain Mapping Group has also published peer-reviewed validations demonstrating CAT12's reliability across different scanner platforms and acquisition parameters [[#ref-4]]([[#ref-4]]).

## Related Software

CAT12 operates within the [[spm]] framework and shares conceptual similarities with [[freesurfer]] for surface-based analysis. For structural connectivity construction, researchers often combine CAT12 outputs with tools like [[mrtrix3-connectome]] or the [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]], using the parcellations and morphometric data to define edge weights representing anatomical coupling between regions. Alternative morphometric approaches include [[brainvoyager]] and [[brainvisa]], while the CAT12 outputs can be visualized using tools like [[mricron]] or integrated into processing pipelines using [[nipype]].
