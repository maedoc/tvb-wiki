## Overview

Gephi is an open-source network visualization platform designed for the interactive exploration and analysis of graph-structured data. In computational neuroscience, such tools serve a critical role within connectomics pipelines, where researchers must interpret high-dimensional connectivity matrices derived from neuroimaging data and evaluate graph-theoretic measures whose meaning shifts depending on whether networks are weighted or binary, directed or undirected [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]] [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]]. By rendering nodes and edges as manipulable objects, visualization platforms enable the inspection of topological features—such as hub regions or community structure—that summary statistics alone may obscure [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]].

## Motivation and Context

Whole-brain modeling and empirical neuroimaging produce adjacency matrices encoding connection strengths between parcellated brain regions, yet these numerical representations resist direct interpretation without visual scaffolding [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. Graph-theoretic measures—including modularity, clustering coefficient, and characteristic path length—characterize network organization, but their interpretation depends fundamentally on whether networks are treated as weighted or binary, directed or undirected [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. Interactive visualization bridges this gap by allowing researchers to explore spatial layouts, filter edges by weight threshold, and detect hub regions or community structure that might otherwise remain hidden in the raw matrix representation [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]].

## Brain Network Visualization

The analysis of brain connectivity relies on a suite of graph measures that quantify network topology, from local clustering to global integration [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. Researchers construct graphs from [[structural-connectivity]] matrices derived via [[diffusion-imaging]] and [[tractography]], or from [[functional-connectivity]] estimates computed from time-series data such as [[fmri]] or [[eeg]] [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. Depending on the research question, these may be represented as weighted or binary, directed or undirected graphs. Visualization platforms complement the computational implementations found in resources like the [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]] by providing an interface for qualitative exploration of the resulting topologies. Such inspection supports the detection of [[small-world-networks]] properties, rich-club organization, and modular community structure in both empirical and simulated networks [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]].

## Relationship to TVB

Gephi serves as a complementary visualization tool for outputs generated by [[the-virtual-brain]] (TVB), a platform for simulating large-scale brain network dynamics [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. TVB integrates empirical [[structural-connectivity]] from diffusion MRI with [[neural-mass-models]] to produce simulated neural activity, which is compared against empirical recordings through forward models for [[fmri]], [[meg]], and [[eeg]] [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. Simulation outputs include anatomical connectivity weights and dynamic correlation matrices; exporting these to external visualization tools enables researchers to verify that simulated networks reproduce expected topological properties such as densely interconnected hubs and modular organization [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. This qualitative inspection represents an important sanity check within the broader [[model-validation]] workflow for [[whole-brain-modeling]] studies.

## Related Software

Multiple toolchains support brain connectivity analysis alongside interactive visualization. [[braph]] offers an open-source MATLAB pipeline for constructing, analyzing, and visualizing brain networks from multimodal neuroimaging including [[fmri]], structural MRI, [[eeg]], and PET [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]]. The [[brain-connectivity-toolbox]] provides a widely used MATLAB implementation of graph-theoretic metrics that can be exported for visualization in external platforms [[raw/papers/rubinov-sporns-2010.md|Rubinov & Sporns (2010)]]. While specialized pipelines such as [[braph]] orient toward statistical group comparisons, general-purpose network visualization tools focus on interactive exploration, reflecting the diversity of methodological approaches in the connectomics community [[raw/papers/mijalkov-2017-braph.md|Mijalkov et al. (2017)]].
