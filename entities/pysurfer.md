PySurfer is a Python library for visualization and analysis of cortical surface data generated by [[freesurfer]]. It provides a programmatic interface for rendering brain surfaces, overlaying statistical maps, displaying ROI labels, and creating publication-quality figures from FreeSurfer anatomical and functional outputs. Built on [[mayavi]]'s 3D rendering engine, PySurfer enables reproducible, scriptable visualizations that avoid the need for manual interaction with FreeSurfer's native GUI tools.

## Motivation and Context

Cortical surface reconstruction has become central to how researchers study brain morphology and geometry in modern neuroimaging [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-97e6ff441097.md|Benn et al. (2025)]]. FreeSurfer is widely used for this reconstruction, producing detailed surfaces, parcellations, and morphological measures from structural MRI. However, traditional visualization in FreeSurfer relies on interactive GUI applications such as [[freeview]], which are poorly suited for batch processing, reproducible pipelines, or integration with automated analysis workflows. Researchers analyzing large imaging cohorts—such as [[hcp-dataset]] releases or [[uk-biobank]] data—require consistent visualizations across hundreds or thousands of subjects without manual intervention. PySurfer addresses this gap by exposing FreeSurfer's surface visualization capabilities through Python, enabling automated figure generation and integration with the broader neuroimaging ecosystem including [[nilearn]], [[mne-python]], and [[pybids]]. The emergence of automated pipelines for cortical surface reconstruction across species further underscores the need for programmatic visualization tools that operate on standard surface formats and reduce technical barriers to scalable surface-based analysis [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-97e6ff441097.md|Benn et al. (2025)]].

## Key Features and Capabilities

PySurfer supports several core capabilities for surface-based neuroimaging analysis. Its primary feature is plotting data on inflated or flattened cortical surfaces with fine-grained control over lighting, colormaps, and annotation labels. Researchers can overlay statistical maps from group analyses, display ROI labels from [[parcellation]] schemes including the [[desikan-killiany-atlas]] or [[glasser-atlas]], and save outputs in vector or raster formats suitable for publication. The library supports multiple simultaneous views for side-by-side hemisphere comparisons and can display curvature information as anatomical background. PySurfer also renders connectivity visualizations, displaying functional or structural connectivity patterns on the cortical surface to aid interpretation of network-level results.

## Relationship to TVB

PySurfer serves as a complementary visualization tool for [[the-virtual-brain]] workflows that involve surface-based brain models. TVB supports cortical surface simulations in which neural activity propagates across regions defined by [[structural-connectivity]] matrices derived from diffusion imaging [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. (2013)]]. The integration of computational modeling with multimodal neuroimaging in TVB emphasizes the importance of high-quality surface visualization for interpreting simulation outputs on realistic cortical geometry [[raw/papers/ritter-2013.md|Ritter et al. (2013)]]. In [[personalized-brain-modeling]] pipelines, TVB instances are parameterized by subject-specific structural connectivity to reproduce individual [[resting-state]] functional patterns [[raw/papers/ritter-2013.md|Ritter et al. (2013)]], and PySurfer provides the visualization layer for rendering individual cortical surfaces, parcellations, and simulation results for quality control and interpretation.

## Related Software

PySurfer occupies a specific niche alongside other surface visualization tools in the neuroimaging landscape. [[nilearn]] provides surface visualization capabilities but focuses primarily on volume-based analyses and statistical inference. [[mne-python]] offers complementary visualization for EEG and MEG source estimation mapped onto cortical surfaces. [[connectome-workbench]] supports command-line visualization for CIFTI format data popular in the [[human-connectome-project]], while [[pycortex]] provides WebGL-based interactive brain visualizations via Python. For pure surface-based statistics, [[surfstat]] offers parallel functionality within MATLAB. Together, these tools form the visualization backbone for modern [[neuroimaging]] research in connectome-based [[whole-brain-modeling]].
