# COINS

## Overview

COINS (Collaborative Informatics Neuroimaging Suite) is a comprehensive, web-based data management system developed at The Mind Research Network (MRN) in Albuquerque, New Mexico. It provides an end-to-end solution for managing neuroimaging and neuropsychology research data, including MRI, MEG, EEG, and genetic data, along with associated phenotypic and clinical assessments. The platform addresses the growing need for sophisticated neuroinformatics systems capable of handling the massive amounts of data generated by modern neuroimaging studies, enabling researchers to store, retrieve, mine, and share data both within and across institutions. COINS implements an open-source model with web-based tools that facilitate study management, subject tracking, imaging data organization, and clinical data collection, making it particularly valuable for large-scale, multi-site research collaborations.

The system emerged from recognition that traditional clinical imaging infrastructure—such as Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS)—while effective in clinical settings, lacks the specialized features required for cognitive neuroscience and neuropsychiatric research. Unlike PACS systems limited to specific imaging modalities, COINS supports the complete spectrum of neuroimaging data types including structural MRI, functional MRI (both task-based and resting-state), diffusion tensor imaging, MEG, and EEG, along with the rich behavioral and clinical assessment data that accompanies neuroimaging studies.

## Key Features

COINS comprises several integrated modules that work together to provide comprehensive data management capabilities. The **Medical Imaging Computer Information System (MICIS)** serves as the central hub for managing studies, subjects, and scan sessions, providing automated compliance tracking for IRB approvals and enrollment limits, subject type classification, and protected health information (PHI) management with audit trails.

The **Assessment Manager** provides web-based tools for creating and managing questionnaires and clinical assessments, supporting conditional jumping, auto-scoring, calculated fields, and dual-entry conflict resolution for data collected on paper. This module includes support for standardized clinical instruments and allows researchers to develop custom assessments tailored to their specific protocols.

The **DICOM Receiver** automatically collects and archives MR imaging data directly from scanners, performing automated integrity checks to ensure data quality before storage. Invalid scans are routed to a temporary location until issues are resolved, maintaining data integrity throughout the acquisition pipeline.

The **Query Builder** enables researchers to search and retrieve data across studies with a user-friendly interface that abstracts away the underlying database complexity. Users can perform cross-study queries, filter by demographic variables, imaging parameters, or assessment scores, and export data in multiple formats including CSV and tab-delimited files.

The **Data Exchange** module extends COINS into a data sharing platform, allowing investigators to share anonymized datasets with the broader research community. It implements fine-grained access controls, Data Usage Agreements (DUA), and supports both pre-approved and request-approved sharing models. The Data Exchange currently hosts several prominent datasets including the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange (ABIDE), the Consortium for Reliability and Reproducibility (CoRR), and the Enhanced NKI Rockland Sample.

## Relationship to TVB

While COINS is primarily a data management and sharing platform rather than a simulation engine, it plays an important role in the whole-brain modeling ecosystem by providing the structural and functional neuroimaging data that researchers use to construct [[connectome]]-based models in tools like [[the-virtual-brain]]. The datasets hosted on COINS Data Exchange—including resting-state fMRI data from ABIDE and CoRR—are frequently employed as inputs for [[structural-connectivity]] matrices used in [[whole-brain-modeling]] simulations. Researchers building personalized brain models in [[tvb]] often require large cohorts of neuroimaging data for [[parameter-estimation]] and validation; COINS facilitates access to such datasets through its standardized data sharing infrastructure. Additionally, COINS-compatible data formats (NIfTI, DICOM) align with TVB's data import requirements, enabling relatively straightforward integration of COINS-managed datasets into TVB workflows for [[personalized-brain-modeling]] research.

## Key Papers

The original COINS system was described by Scott et al. (2011) in Frontiers in Neuroinformatics, which detailed the architecture and capabilities of the tool suite built for large heterogeneous datasets. This initial publication compared COINS to XNAT and outlined how the system addresses seven key challenges in neuroimaging data sharing: PHI management, in situ versus ex situ sharing, standardized metadata versus extensibility, intuitive ease of use, user-friendly query interfaces, search speed and scalability, and data provenance tracking.

Subsequent developments were documented in King et al. (2014), which focused on automated collection of imaging and phenotypic data to centralized and distributed data repositories. Wood et al. (2014) described the modern web technologies employed for creating intuitive data visualization and sharing tools within the COINS framework.

The COINS Data Exchange platform was formally introduced by Landis et al. (2015) in Neuroimage, presenting the three-pronged approach of data compilation, curation, and dissemination that enables large-scale neuroimaging data sharing. The paper reported that Data Exchange had packaged over 31 TB of data from nearly 1,200 unique data requests, demonstrating the platform's utility in facilitating community-wide data reuse.

## Relationship to Similar Systems

COINS shares significant functional overlap with [[xnat]] (Extensible Neuroimaging Archive Toolkit), another widely-used neuroimaging data management system. Both platforms provide subject and study management, imaging data archival, query capabilities, and data sharing features. However, several distinguishing factors differentiate the two systems. COINS emphasizes intuitive ease of use for non-technical users and provides integrated assessment management alongside imaging data—a capability not native to XNAT. The systems also differ in their approach to metadata extensibility, with COINS using an entity-attribute-value (EAV) model through its Assessment Manager to allow end-user extensibility without database administration support.

Unlike purely archival systems, COINS is designed from the ground up to support the complete research lifecycle from study design and participant enrollment through data collection, quality control, analysis, and eventual data sharing. This end-to-end approach distinguishes it from point solutions that address only specific components of the research data management pipeline.

## Data Standards and interoperability

COINS supports multiple neuroimaging data formats including DICOM, NIfTI, and various derivative formats appropriate for different analysis pipelines. The system stores imaging data in de-identified form on RAID storage systems while maintaining metadata about the original DICOM headers in the database. This separation allows researchers to access both the raw imaging data and the rich metadata needed for filtering and quality control without exposing personally identifiable information. Data providers can choose their preferredsharing format, and the system performs automated header anonymization to remove identifying information before data distribution. The Data Exchange supports a wide range of file types including .bval, .bvec, .csv, .dcm, .edat, .gz, .hdr, .img, .mat, .nii, .nrrd, and .zip archives, providing flexibility for different analysis workflows.

## Institutional Deployment and Scale

As of the most recent reports, COINS manages data from over 300 studiesacross nine institutions, containing more than 14,000 subjects and 19,000 MRI, MEG, and EEG scan sessions in addition to over 180,000 clinical assessments. The Mind Research Network serves as the primary hosting site, though the architecture supports distributed deployment models where institutions can maintain local data storage while participating in centralized metadata catalogs. This hybrid approach—sometimes described as "centralized metadata with distributed data"—allows institutions to retain full control over their raw imaging data while benefiting from COINS's metadata management and sharing capabilities.
