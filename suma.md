SUMA (Surface Modeling System) is a specialized software toolbox developed at the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) for surface-based visualization and analysis of neuroimaging data. Designed as a complement to the [[afni]] suite, SUMA enables researchers to project volumetric data—such as [[fmri]] activation maps and connectivity matrices—onto triangulated cortical surface models, revealing spatial patterns that conventional volume renders may obscure due to cortical folding. While whole-brain modeling platforms such as [[the-virtual-brain]] simulate primate brain network dynamics using biologically realistic structural connectivity to generate macroscopic neuroimaging signals [[raw/papers/sanz-leon-2013.md|Sanz Leon et al. 2013]], SUMA operates downstream as a visualization and post-processing layer that bridges simulated outputs with anatomically accurate surface representations. The expanding ecosystem of cortical reconstruction tools underscores the growing significance of surface-based analysis: species-agnostic pipelines now extend FreeSurfer-compatible surface generation to non-human datasets [[raw/papers/semanticscholar-97e6ff441097.md|Benn et al. 2025]], and integrated MEG/EEG analysis platforms incorporate anatomical visualization and source localization within unified graphical environments [[raw/papers/arxiv-2604.16463.md|Liu 2026]]. In this context, SUMA functions as an interoperability node linking [[whole-brain-modeling]] simulations and empirical neuroimaging data with surface-based anatomical frameworks, supporting direct comparison between simulated and observed activation patterns.

## Overview

SUMA (Surface Modeling System) is a specialized software toolbox for surface-based visualization and analysis of neuroimaging data, developed and maintained by the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH). Originally conceived as a complement to AFNI (Analysis of Functional NeuroImages), SUMA enables researchers to display volumetric neuroimaging data—such as fMRI activation maps—onto triangulated cortical surface models generated by FreeSurfer or similar segmentation tools. The software provides a unified environment for examining both volume-based and surface-based data, facilitating the interpretation of functional imaging results in the context of anatomically realistic cortical anatomy.

## Key Features

SUMA provides several capabilities that distinguish it from pure volume-based visualization tools. First, it supports the import of surfaces generated by FreeSurfer, allowing researchers to display statistical maps on inflated or flattened cortical patches. This approach reveals activation patterns that may be obscured in standard volume renders due to cortical folding. Second, SUMA enables the mapping of time-series data onto surfaces, supporting the examination of dynamic connectivity patterns and resting-state networks at the mesh level. Third, the toolbox includes ROI-based analysis tools, allowing users to extract average statistical values from anatomically defined regions and perform group comparisons. Finally, SUMA integrates closely with AFNI, such that processing pipelines can move seamlessly between volume-based statistical analysis in AFNI and surface-based visualization in SUMA without manual format conversion.

The software operates through a controller-module architecture: one launches the graphical controller to manage multiple display modules, each of which can show different surfaces, volumes, or overlay maps. This modular design supports the simultaneous visualization of multiple subjects, group averages, and cross-subject alignment using SUMA's own surface registration tools (e.g., @SUMA_Make_Spec_FS and map-based resampling to standard meshes such as fsaverage).

## Relationship to TVB

While [[the-virtual-brain]] (TVB) focuses on whole-brain computational modeling and simulation of neural dynamics, SUMA serves primarily as a visualization and post-processing tool for empirical neuroimaging data. Researchers using TVB to simulate fMRI signals—typically by coupling neural mass models to a hemodynamic model—may employ SUMA to visualize the resulting statistical maps on anatomically reconstructed cortical surfaces. The integration typically proceeds as follows: TVB produces NIfTI-format volume files containing simulated BOLD signals or connectivity matrices; these volumes are then loaded into AFNI for statistical analysis; finally, the statistical maps are projected onto FreeSurfer-defined surfaces and displayed in SUMA for surface-based inspection. This workflow bridges the gap between [[whole-brain-modeling]] and conventional neuroimaging analysis, enabling direct comparison between simulated and empirical activation patterns.

## Technical Implementation

SUMA handles several surface mesh formats, including the GIFTI format endorsed by the AFNI/SUMA community and the legacy VRML-based formats from FreeSurfer. The software implements accurate volume-to-surface mapping using nearest-neighbor interpolation or Gaussian weighting, allowing users to specify the radius of contribution from each vertex to the underlying volume data. For group analysis, SUMA supports surface-based resampling to a common mesh (e.g., the fsaverage mesh), enabling vertex-wise cross-subject statistics while accounting for inter-subject anatomical variability.

## Key Papers

The primary reference for SUMA is the original technical description by Ziad S. Saad and colleagues (2006), which outlines the software's integration with AFNI and its surface handling capabilities. Subsequent methodological papers have demonstrated SUMA's utility in connectivity-focused analyses, showing how surface-based representation can improve the interpretation of resting-state networks identified through [[functional-connectivity]] analyses of fMRI data.

## Related Software

SUMA is intimately connected to the broader neuroimaging software ecosystem. It is distributed alongside [[afni]] as part of the NIMH's open-source neuroimaging suite. The software interfaces with [[freesurfer]] for surface generation, [[connectome-workbench]] for additional group-level surface visualization, and standard volumetric tools such as [[fsl]] and [[spm]] for preprocessing. Alternative surface visualization platforms include [[brainnet-viewer]] and [[brainstorm]], though these operate independently of AFNI's processing framework.
