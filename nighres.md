# Nighres

## Overview

Nighres (NIH High-Resolution) is a Python-based open-source software toolbox designed specifically for processing high-resolution neuroimaging data, with a particular emphasis on laminar and columnar cortical imaging. Developed primarily at the National Institutes of Health (NIH), Nighres provides a comprehensive suite of tools for extracting, analyzing, and visualizing cortical microstructure from ultra-high-field (7 Tesla and above) MRI data. The toolbox targets researchers studying the laminar architecture of the cerebral cortex—the six-layered structure that forms the histological basis of cortical computation—and enables quantitative analysis of myelin content, cortical depth profiles, and columnar organizations across the cortical sheet.

## Motivation and Context

The emergence of ultra-high-field MRI (7T and beyond) has opened unprecedented opportunities for non-invasive investigation of cortical microstructure in vivo. Unlike conventional 3T MRI, high-field systems can achieve sufficient spatial resolution to resolve cortical layers (typically 0.5–1.0 mm isotropic or better), enabling researchers to probe the columnar and laminar machinery of the cortex without resorting to invasive histological methods. However, the analysis of such high-resolution data presents novel methodological challenges that standard neuroimaging pipelines—designed for whole-brain imaging at 1–2 mm resolution—are ill-equipped to handle.

Nighres emerged to address this methodological gap. Standard tools like [[freesurfer]] and [[fsl]] are optimized for voxel sizes around 1mm and often require substantial modification or produce suboptimal results when applied to high-resolution laminar data. The toolbox was developed to provide end-to-end processing capabilities specifically tailored to the unique characteristics of high-resolution structural and quantitative MRI, including specialized algorithms for cortical layer segmentation, intensity normalization across cortical depth, and extraction of depth-dependent metrics such as myelin content estimates.

## Key Features

Nighres provides several core processing capabilities that distinguish it from general-purpose neuroimaging toolboxes. First, the toolbox implements sophisticated cortical layer segmentation algorithms that can reconstruct the cortical ribbon from high-resolution data and subdivide it into multiple equidistant laminae (typically 5–10 layers) from the pial surface to the white matter interface. This layering approach enables researchers to sample MRI signal properties at consistent fractional depths across the cortex, facilitating cross-subject comparisons of laminar-specific features.

Second, Nighres includes tools for quantitative myelin mapping based on T1-weighted/T2-weighted ratio analysis and magnetisation transfer imaging. These methods allow for the estimation of myelin content as a function of cortical depth, revealing the characteristic dip in myelin concentration in layer IV (the internal granular layer) that is a hallmark of isocortex. Third, the toolbox provides columnar segmentation routines that can identify and analyze periodic columnar structures observable in certain high-resolution imaging contrasts, particularly in primary sensory and motor cortices.

The software is built atop [[nipype]] for pipeline construction, ensuring compatibility with existing neuroimaging workflows and enabling integration with tools like [[freesurfer]], [[ants]], and [[fsl]] for preprocessing and registration. Nighres operates on NIfTI format data and outputs processed layers, surfaces, and statistical maps that can be visualized using tools like [[freeview]], [[fsleyes]], or [[brainnet-viewer]].

## Relationship to TVB

While Nighres operates primarily in the preprocessing and feature extraction domain rather than simulation, it provides valuable anatomical constraints for [[whole-brain-modeling]] efforts in [[the-virtual-brain]]. The laminar profiles and myelin maps generated by Nighres can inform the assignment of region-specific parameters in neural mass models (such as the [[jansen-rit-model]] or [[wong-wang-model]]) that distinguish between primary sensory areas (characterized by heavy thalamocortical input to layer IV) and higher-order association cortices. Furthermore, the high-resolution parcellations produced by Nighres can refine the [[structural-connectivity]] matrices derived from [[diffusion-imaging]] and [[tractography]] by providing more accurate estimates of cortical boundaries and laminar connectivity patterns.

## Related Software

Nighres intersects with several other software tools in the high-resolution neuroimaging ecosystem. It complements [[freesurfer]] by providing layer-specific analysis capabilities beyond the standard cortical thickness measurements. The toolbox can be used in conjunction with [[ants]] for registration and normalization, and with [[mrtrix3]] for advanced [[diffusion-imaging]] analysis. For visualization, outputs can be rendered in [[brainnet-viewer]] or exported to formats compatible with [[connectome-workbench]]. Related projects include [[brainsuite]] for surface-based processing and [[mindboggle]] for automated labeling of cortical structures.

## Technical Considerations

Several practical aspects of Nighres warrant attention for prospective users. Processing high-resolution (sub-millimeter) data demands substantial computational resources—both in terms of memory and processing time—and the file sizes can be orders of magnitude larger than conventional neuroimaging datasets. Additionally, the quality of laminar segmentations depends critically on the quality of the input data; susceptibility artifacts near air-tissue boundaries (particularly in orbitofrontal and temporal regions) can compromise segmentation accuracy. Users should also be aware that the biological interpretation of quantitative myelin maps requires careful consideration of partial volume effects and the relationship between MRI signal and underlying histology.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

The field of laminar imaging remains methodologically active, and Nighres continues to evolve. Open questions include the optimal number of layers for capturing biologically meaningful variation, the validation of in vivo laminar measures against postmortem histology, and the development of standardized pipelines for multi-site, multi-scanner harmonization. As ultra-high-field MRI becomes more widely available, tools like Nighres will likely play an increasingly important role in bridging the gap between macroscale neuroimaging and microscale cortical biology.
