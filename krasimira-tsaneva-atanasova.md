# Krasimira Tsaneva-Atanasova

Krasimira Tsaneva-Atanasova is a researcher specializing in [[computational-neuroscience]], [[connectome]]-based [[whole-brain modeling]], and the mathematical analysis of [[neural-mass-model]]s. Her work bridges dynamical systems theory with neuroimaging, focusing on how large-scale brain networks generate coherent activity patterns and how these patterns relate to both healthy brain function and neurological disorders.

## Affiliations

Krasimira Tsaneva-Atanasova is associated with research groups advancing [[whole-brain modeling]] and [[neural-mass-model]] development. Her work connects to the broader ecosystem of researchers developing tools like [[TVB]] and contributes to international efforts in [[computational-psychiatry]] applications. She collaborates with researchers in the field of [[brain-dynamics]] and [[network-dynamics]], particularly those employing mathematical frameworks from [[dynamical-systems-theory]] to understand brain function.

## Research Focus

Tsaneva-Atanasova's research centers on the intersection of [[structural-connectivity]] and [[functional-connectivity]] in the human brain. Using [[whole-brain-modeling]] approaches, she investigates how anatomical pathways—measured through [[diffusion-imaging]] and [[tractography]]—constrain the emergence of functional brain dynamics. Her work employs [[neural-mass-model]]s that represent populations of neurons as coupled oscillators, capturing the mesoscopic activity patterns observable in [[fMRI]], [[EEG]], and [[MEG]] recordings.

A key aspect of her research involves [[parameter-estimation]] for personalized brain models. By fitting [[neural-mass-model]] parameters to individual neuroimaging data, her work contributes to the goal of [[personalized-brain-modeling]]—creating digital twins of individual brains that can be used to predict disease progression, treatment response, or the effects of [[brain-stimulation]] interventions. This approach is particularly relevant for [[epilepsy-modeling]], where personalized models may help identify seizure onset zones and optimize surgical planning.

Her theoretical work draws on [[bifurcation-theory]] to understand how brain networks transition between different dynamical regimes—how the brain maintains stability yet exhibits flexible transitions between rest, task, and pathological states. This places her research within the framework of [[nonlinear-dynamics]] and [[network-dynamics]], where small changes in parameters can lead to dramatic shifts in network behavior.

## Relationships and Collaborative Context

The field of [[whole-brain modeling]] is inherently collaborative, spanning neuroscience, mathematics, computer science, and clinical research. Tsaneva-Atanasova's work connects to several key researchers and platforms in the field. The research group around Viktor Jirsa and colleagues at the University of Amsterdam has developed major frameworks for [[whole-brain modeling]], including the [[epileptor]] model for seizure modeling and the TVB platform. Additionally, researchers like Gustavo Deco and Morten Kringelholm have advanced our understanding of how [[whole-brain]] models can capture task-related and resting-state [[functional-connectivity]] patterns.

Her work also relates to the broader [[connectomics]] effort, exemplified by projects like the [[hcp-dataset]], which provides high-quality structural and functional neuroimaging data essential for validating [[whole-brain model]]s. The relationship between [[structural-connectivity]] and [[functional-connectivity]] remains a central question in the field, with Tsaneva-Atanasova's research contributing to this ongoing scientific conversation.

## Open Questions

The field of [[whole-brain modeling]] faces several open questions that Tsaneva-Atanasova's work addresses. The challenge of [[parameter-estimation]]—identifying the biophysically plausible parameters that reproduce individual brain dynamics—remains computationally intensive and theoretically challenging. How to best integrate [[effective-connectivity]] measures (which infer causal interactions) with [[structural-connectivity]] (which provides anatomical constraints) is an active area of research. Finally, the translation of personalized brain models to clinical applications, including [[epilepsy-modeling]] and [[schizophrenia-models]], requires further validation against longitudinal patient data.
