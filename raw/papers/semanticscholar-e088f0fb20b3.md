## Abstract

Accurate diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease (AD) is vital for enabling early intervention and slowing disease progression. Deep learning methods based on structural magnetic resonance imaging (sMRI) have emerged as a powerful tool for automated AD diagnosis. However, significant challenges remain in effectively capturing the subtle and heterogeneous patterns of brain atrophy characteristic of AD, particularly in distinguishing them from changes associated with normal aging. To address these challenges, we propose a novel three-dimensional convolutional neural network (3D-CNN) for AD classification. The core innovation of our model lies in the integration of two bespoke modules: (1) a Residual Dense Multi-Scale Block (RDMS), which utilizes parallel convolutional kernels of varying sizes combined with dense and residual connections. This is designed to simultaneously capture multi-scale patterns of brain atrophy, ranging from local details to regional morphological changes; and (2) a 3D Coordinate Attention module (CoordAtt3D), which encodes precise spatial information to capture direction-aware features and long-range dependencies. This enables the model to adaptively focus on key brain regions that contribute most significantly to diagnosis, such as the hippocampus and temporal lobes. We evaluated our proposed model on the publicly available Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that our model achieved an accuracy of 91.34% in the binary classification of AD versus cognitively normal (CN) subjects. Our study confirms that the proposed strategy, which synergizes multi-scale feature fusion with 3D spatial attention, can significantly enhance the performance of automated AD diagnosis, presenting an effective and reliable auxiliary tool for potential clinical application.
