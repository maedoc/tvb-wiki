## Abstract

The growth of neuromorphic computing has enabled the development of efficient and biologically inspired computational models, with Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) emerging as a promising approach. Unlike conventional neural models, SNNs represent neurons and synaptic interactions in a manner closer to biological processes, using discrete spikes to transmit information. This paper presents a detailed methodology for implementing Spiking Neural Networks on neuromorphic hardware, describing synaptic connectivity, spike generation mechanisms, and the evaluation of network architectures for energy-efficient operation. Neuromorphic chips are uniquely designed to support the event-driven, lowpower operations of SNNs, making them suitable for applications requiring real-time and energy-efficient processing. Our methodology includes configuring neuron models, establishing spike-based synaptic interactions, generating spike trains from input stimuli, and incorporating Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP) to enable learning. The implemented SNN is simulated using the Brian2 framework and evaluated on the MNIST dataset, achieving 91.66% accuracy. Using recent Loihi 2 benchmarks from literature, we extrapolate the potential energy and latency benefits when deploying such SNNs on neuromorphic chips, underscoring their applicability in robotics, sensory processing, and edge computing.
