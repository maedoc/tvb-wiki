## Abstract

In existing EEG signal processing applications, it is generally assumed that different brain regions exhibit consistent connectivity patterns under identical scenarios, with variations attributable to individual or environmental differences. However, recent advances in neuroscience have revealed that functional connectivity (FC) within the brain is inherently dynamic and varies continuously, even for the same individual under identical or continuous scenarios. Current studies often represent inter-regional FC using correlation matrices derived from EEG channels; however, these are typically precomputed as static matrices or iteratively updated under static assumptions. While emerging medical research leverages dynamic FC from fMRI for brain disorder classification, fMRI’s low temporal resolution of fMRI limits its applicability in real-time tasks, such as driver fatigue detection or motor intent recognition. This study constructs dynamic FC features using Pearson correlation coefficients from EEG signals and validates their efficacy in emotion recognition and driver fatigue scenarios, demonstrating significant performance improvements, although good results were obtained in the application of time; no significant differences were directly found in the direct comparison with static brain connections. Therefore, we believe that dynamic brain connections can only be used as prior knowledge of graphs and not as a separate feature to perform classification or recognition tasks.
