## Abstract

This paper presents a hardware design of a Spiking Neural Network (SNN) based on the Izhikevich neuron model for handwritten digit recognition. The proposed architecture comprises three main layers (including input, hidden, and output), integrated with unsupervised learning through Spike-Timing Dependent Plasticity (STDP) and a Winner-Take-All (WTA) mechanism for effective feature extraction and classification. An Address-Event Representation (AER) bus is employed to optimize spike communication and mitigate connectivity bottlenecks within the network. The MNIST dataset is adopted for network training and performance evaluation. The physical implementation achieves a final layout area of $11.87 \text{mm}^{2}$ with a power consumption of 4.5 W, operating at a maximum frequency of 100 MHz. Experimental evaluation demonstrates recognition accuracy ranging from $\mathbf{7 5 \%}$ to $\mathbf{9 0 \%}$ across different digit classes for input images of $12 \times 12$ pixels, confirming the effectiveness of the proposed SNN hardware design. Each input image requires 5.6 ms for processing before producing the corresponding network output. This work demonstrates the capability of the SNN to serve as a powerful framework for neuromorphic computing while offering high efficiency in pattern recognition tasks.
