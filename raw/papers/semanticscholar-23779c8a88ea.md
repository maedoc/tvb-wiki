## Abstract

Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), as a non-invasive imaging technique, has been widely applied in the automated analysis of brain disorders. However, most existing fMRI-based methods overlook the modeling of cross-scale coupling mechanisms of brain activity as well as interhemispheric functional integration. Spiking neural networks (SNNs), with their biologically plausible spike-based encoding and event-driven characteristics, provide an efficient and interpretable framework for multi-scale dynamic representations of fMRI signals. Never-theless, current SNN approaches still face significant limitations in handling dynamic interhemispheric interactions. To address these issues, we propose a biologically inspired framework for the diagnosis of brain disorders that integrates a Multi-Timescale Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (MT-LIF) encoding module with hemispheric fusion and coupling strategies. By maintaining dual membrane potentials, continuous fMRI signals are transformed into discrete spike trains, thereby capturing both transient high-frequency activities and slow low-frequency evolutions. Moreover, leveraging the anatomical symmetry of the brain, we introduce bilinear pooling to model second-order cross-hemispheric interactions, and employ Hadamard-product-based coupling to extract intra-regional cross-frequency dynamics at both fast and slow timescales. Experiments on a real epilepsy dataset demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing methods across multiple evaluation metrics. Beyond improving classification performance, it also offers a novel technical pathway and a physiologically interpretable framework for the diagnostic analysis of brain disorders.
