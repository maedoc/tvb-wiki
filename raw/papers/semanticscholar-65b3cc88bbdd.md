## Abstract

Constructing brain networks from resting-state functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (rs-fMRI) has proven to be an effective approach for assisting in the diagnosis of psychiatric disorders. While deep neural networks have been widely adopted for brain network classification due to their ability to automatically learn hierarchical features, most existing methods rely heavily on large amounts of labeled data. However, in real-world scenarios, acquiring labeled brain network data is both costly and time-consuming, leading to a typical small-sample learning challenge. To bridge this gap between data requirements and availability, we propose a novel Graph Laplacian Semi-Supervised Deep Hashing Learning (GL-SSDHL) method that leverages abundant unlabeled data to improve classification performance under limited supervision. The proposed GL-SSDHL framework comprises three key stages: (1) unsupervised feature learning through a Graph Laplacian Variational Autoencoder (GL-VAE), (2) parameter transfer via a shared network architecture, and (3) classification via a semi-supervised deep hashing learning module. Experimental results on the ABDIE I dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of our method: when only 5% of the data is labeled, GL-SSDHL improves the average classification accuracy (based on ten-fold cross-validation) by 4.65% compared to BrainNetCNN and by 8.17% compared to BNC-DGHL, indicating superior performance under low-resource conditions.
