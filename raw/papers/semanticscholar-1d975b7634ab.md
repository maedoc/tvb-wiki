## Abstract

Glaucoma is classically considered as an ophthalmological disease and it is characterized by optic nerve atrophy and loss of visual
field. However, advanced data confirm that glaucoma is a multifactorial neurodegenerative disease affecting brain areas beyond the
visual system. The review is focused on the analysis of literature revealing possibilities of brain neuroimaging in glaucoma patients.
Routine structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) reveals nonspecific changes in various parts of the visual pathway associated with
glaucoma. In contrast, advanced neuroimaging techniques offer new possibilities in diagnosis and monitoring of the glaucoma process.
Magnetic resonance morphometry provides quantitative and qualitative assessment of gray matter atrophy both in the visual cortex
and beyond. Diffusion tensor imaging methods may detect microstructural damage to white matter associated with axonal damage
and destruction of the myelin sheath associated with glaucoma. Various functional neuroimaging techniques (e. g., functional MRI and
magnetic resonance spectroscopy) make it possible to assess functional and metabolic state of various parts of the visual pathway
and have a high potential in determining brain abnormalities in patients with glaucoma. A multimodal approach integrating structural
and functional MRI data with clinical ophthalmological ones significantly expands understanding of glaucoma as a neurodegenerative
disease of the central nervous system and opens up new prospects for early diagnosis, biomarker identification and future development
of targeted therapy.
