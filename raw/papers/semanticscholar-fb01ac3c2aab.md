## Abstract

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a prevalent neurodevelopmental condition characterized by inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity. Traditional diagnostic practices rely heavily on subjective assessments, which may lead to inconsistencies. Electroencephalography (EEG) offers a non-invasive and objective alternative. This paper introduces a novel EEG-based framework for ADHD classification using Efficient Effective Connectivity (EEC) features, a refined form of directional brain connectivity. We compare EEC with the conventional Partial Directed Coherence (PDC) method, evaluating both using a 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). The proposed EEC-based model achieves a classification accuracy of 82.73% ± 1.46 over 10-fold cross-validations, outperforming PDC (68.34% ± 1.95). These results demonstrate the potential of EEC in enhancing objective, data-driven ADHD classification.
