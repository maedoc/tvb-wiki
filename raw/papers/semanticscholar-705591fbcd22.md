## Abstract

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have demonstrated potential to recreate the elaborate structure of human brain connectivity, but their application thus far has remained limited because of generalization issues, use of only homogeneous data, utilization of unimodal neuroimaging data, and lack of interpretability to support clinical translation. In this study, we present Multimodal Explainable Graph Neural Networks for Generalizable and Clinically Trustworthy Brain Connectivity Analysis (MEG-GTC), a paradigm that addresses these issues by jointly encoding structural MRI, functional MRI, and diffusion tensor imaging into common graph representations that encode complementary anatomical and functional aspects of the brain. MEG-GTC is made robust by training on a wide range of multi-site data and using domain adaptation techniques that address demographic and imaging-protocol variability. Moreover, the framework uses hierarchical attention and graph based attribution to deliver insights in the form of transparent, clinically interpretable information on the connectivity patterns that underlie predictions. On a range of neurological and psychiatric conditions, MEG-GTC demonstrates high performance relative to unimodal and non-explainable baselines and yields clinically meaningful biomarkers. The paper paves the way to more accurate neuroscience by resolving the divide between AI models that are high-performing and those that are clinically reliable in their decision support.
