## Abstract

Enterprise networks face escalating threats from sophisticated cyber-attacks, necessitating advanced monitoring systems capable of real-time detection and low-latency responses. This study presents a novel approach to enterprise network threat monitoring using Deep Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), which mimic biological neural processing to enhance efficiency and temporal sensitivity. The proposed framework preprocesses network traffic into spatiotemporal representations and feeds them into an SNN model trained on labeled threat datasets. The methodology leverages spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) and event-driven computation, allowing for energy-efficient inference suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments. Experimental evaluations on benchmark network intrusion datasets demonstrate that the SNN model achieves competitive accuracy with significantly lower computational overhead than conventional deep learning approaches. Results highlight improved detection of temporal attack patterns such as DDoS and port scanning. In conclusion, Deep SNNs offer a promising direction for scalable, low-power enterprise network threat detection.
