## Abstract


 Criticality in the stochastic Wilson-Cowan model has received considerable attention, but how this model system tunes itself to the vicinity of the critical point remains unclear. To address this interesting issue, we study an extended version of the stochastic Wilson–Cowan model, which incorporates short-term synaptic plasticity for both excitatory and inhibitory neurons. By analyzing the phase diagram of the corresponding mean-field equations and characterizing the properties of the attractors, we identify a dynamical regime where self-organized quasi-criticality coexists with stochastic oscillations, a novel mechanism for understanding collective neural activity. Moreover, we demonstrate the coexistence of scale-free avalanches and “King” avalanches within this regime. In addition, we show that there exists a bistable region when the synaptic recovery time of excitatory neurons is relatively short. These results reveal that synaptic plasticity plays a pivotal role in shaping complex neuronal dynamics.
