## Abstract

Objective: Precise tip positioning of Peripherally Inserted Central Catheters (PICC) is crucial for ensuring infusion safety and reducing complications. While the intracardiac electrocardiogram (ECG) positioning method guides catheter tip placement by monitoring P-wave amplitude changes, its clinical application is limited by the subjectivity and experience dependence of manual interpretation. This study aimed to develop a deep learning-based automatic analysis system for intracardiac ECGs to achieve real-time, objective, and high-precision PICC tip positioning, providing technical support for intelligent nursing. Methods:Dataset Construction: Data were collected from 200 PICC patients in the oncology and cardiovascular departments of a tertiary B hospital, including intracardiac ECG signals (sampling rate: 1000 Hz, frequency band: 0.05–100 Hz) and synchronized digital subtraction angiography (DSA) gold-standard positioning results. Data annotation, including P-wave onset, peak amplitude, morphological changes (e.g., bifid, notched), and final catheter tip location (lower one-third of the superior vena cava as standard), was independently performed by three senior ultrasonographers. Model Design: A hybrid convolutional neural network (CNN) and long short-term memory network (LSTM) (CNN-LSTM) was employed, where CNN extracted time-frequency features of intracardiac ECGs, and LSTM captured dynamic P-wave changes. A spatial attention mechanism was introduced to enhance focus on critical P-wave segments (e.g., 0.1–0.3 s post-QRS) and suppress noise (e.g., baseline drift). Multi-task learning simultaneously outputted catheter tip location (classification task: superior vena cava/right atrium/other) and P-wave morphological abnormality alerts (binary classification task: normal/abnormal). Validation Metrics: Positioning accuracy was evaluated using Euclidean distance error between model predictions and DSA gold standards. Clinical efficacy was compared with traditional manual intracardiac ECG interpretation (82% accuracy) and surface positioning (76% accuracy). Real-time performance was tested on an embedded device (NVIDIA Jetson TX2) in terms of inference speed (frames/second). Results:Positioning Accuracy: The model achieved 97.8% classification accuracy with an average Euclidean distance error of 3.2 mm in the test set, significantly outperforming traditional methods (8.5–12 mm error, P < 0.01). Sensitivity and specificity reached 94.2% and 93.7%, respectively. For complex cases (e.g., patients with cardiac pacemakers or arrhythmias), accuracy improved to 89.5% (vs. 67.3% for traditional methods). Clinical Benefits:Operational Efficiency: Single-case positioning time reduced to 8 minutes (vs. 15-20 minutes traditionally), with a 50% shorter nurse learning curve.Complication Reduction: Postoperative catheter malposition rates decreased from 24% to 4.1%, and thrombus incidence dropped by 72%. Radiation Exposure: Complete replacement of X-ray screening reduced patient radiation dose by 90%. Technical Innovations:Dynamic Adaptation: Real-time tissue impedance monitoring (>800 Ω indicating catheter adhesion) enabled automatic model parameter adjustment for individual differences. Lightweight Deployment: Model parameters were compressed to 8.5 MB, enabling real-time operation on portable ultrasound devices (e.g., Mindray M9) at 35 frames/second. Conclusion: This study developed an automatic intracardiac ECG analysis system using deep learning, achieving sub-millimeter PICC tip positioning accuracy. Clinical validation demonstrated significant reductions in operational risks and efficiency improvements, providing an innovative solution for intelligent vascular access management. Future plans include integrating federated learning to construct a multi-center catheter positioning database for enhanced model generalization.
