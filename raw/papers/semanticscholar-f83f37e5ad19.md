## Abstract

autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder that is characterized by impaired communication and social interaction. Conventional diagnostic methods generally depend on behaviour observation, and clinical practices, which do not always receive priority due to their subjective assessment, are time-consuming and do not allow early diagnosis. Recent developments in neuroimaging and artificial intelligence research have encouraged the composition of EEG with deep learning techniques for the objective, data-driven diagnosis. In this work, multimodal EEG-Deep learning model for the classification of ASD and non-ASD that overcomes the limitations like small dataset, demographical bias and non-medical utility observed in previous research. For this reason, the authors have collected all the data from Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange (ABIDE). The authors proposed a novel multimodal relevance-constrained transfer learning model that combines different types of brain features like time–frequency decomposition, entropy measures, and connectivity maps and multimodal fusion methods to preserve both spectral and spatial properties in the brain. Deep learning models, particularly CNNs with and without attention mechanism were used for automatic feature extraction and classification. In order to prevent overfitting, dropout, L2-regularization and EEG-specific data augmentation were used. Cross-validation by subject enabled generalization and stability across subjects. Performance metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, F1-score and area under curve (AUC) were computed by stratified evaluation. The proposed model reached overall classification accuracy of 99.45%, and with sensitivity and specificity higher than 98%, which performs better over traditional machine learning baseline like SVM, KNN. Theverdictsspecifies that multimodal EEG DL is a robust and interpretable method for early ASD diagnosis, that represents the potential to be combined into clinical settings.
