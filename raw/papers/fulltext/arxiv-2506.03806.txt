                                                                                                ABSTRACT
                                                             This paper classifies complex local representations of the twisted virtual braid group,
                                                          TVB2 , into GL3 (C). It shows that such representations fall into eight types, all of which
                                                          are unfaithful and reducible to degree 2 × 2. Further reducibility to degree 1 is analyzed
                                                          for specific types. The paper also examines complex homogeneous local representations
                                                          of TVBn into GLn+1 (C) for n ≥ 3, identifying seven unfaithful types. Additionally,
                                                          complex local representations of the singular twisted virtual braid group, STVB2 , into
                                                          M3 (C) are classified into thirteen unfaithful types. Finally, the paper demonstrates that
                                                          not all complex local extensions of TVB2 representations to STVB2 conform to a Φ-type
                                                          extension.




                                                     1. Introduction
                                                     The braid group Bn on n strands was introduced by Emil Artin in 1925 [1]. It is
                                                     defined as an abstract group generated by the elements σ1 , σ2 , . . . , σn−1 , subject
                                                     to specific relations that model the fundamental topological behavior of braiding.
                                                     These groups play a crucial role in low-dimensional topology, algebra, and mathe-
                                                     matical physics. In 2004, Louis Kauffman and Sofia Lambropoulou [12] introduced
                                                     the virtual braid group V Bn , which generalizes the classical braid group by incor-
                                                     porating virtual crossings. Like Bn , it is defined by generators and relations and
                                                     forms the algebraic foundation of virtual knot theory. Building upon this, in 2023,
                                                     Komal Negi, Madeti Prabhakar, and Seiichi Kamada [23] introduced the twisted
                                                     virtual braid group T V Bn , which further extends V Bn by including new generators

         and relations that model twists in virtual braids. This provides a more nuanced al-
         gebraic structure capable of representing additional knot-theoretic phenomena.

             Earlier, in 1992 and 1993, John Baez [2] and Joan Birman [7] separately in-
         troduced the singular braid monoid SMn , a generalization of the braid group that
         allows singular crossings, interpreted as collisions of strands. This structure is again
         presented in terms of generators and relations. Most recently, in 2025, Madeti Prab-
         hakar and Komal Negi [25] introduced the singular twisted virtual braid monoid
         ST V Bn , which unifies the ideas of singularity and twisting in the context of virtual
         braids. They also defined a group structure denoted by ST V Gn , into which ST V Bn
         can be embedded. This new algebraic object offers a rich framework for studying
         generalizations of braids in virtual and singular settings.

             The Lawrence–Krammer–Bigelow representation of Bn has been proven faith-
         ful for all n, establishing the linearity of the braid group. Yu. Mikhalchishina [17]
         introduced local linear representations of B3 and homogeneous 2-local representa-
         tions of Bn for n ≥ 2. These results were extended by Taher Mayassi and Mo-
         hamad Nasser [19], who classified all homogeneous 3-local representations of the
         braid group Bn for n ≥ 4.

             In [3], Valeriy Bardakov, Nafaa Chibili, and Tatyana Kozlovskaya introduced the
         concept of Φ-type extensions of representations of Bn to SMn . Mohamad Nasser
         studied the faithfulness of these representation in some cases [20]. This idea was
         further developed by Madeti Prabhakar and Komal Negi [25], who extended it to
         the twisted virtual setting, from T V Bn to ST V Bn . The well-known Birman repre-
         sentation, which is faithfully shown by Luis Paris [24] —is a special case of a Φ-type
         extension of a representation of Bn to SMn , which confirms that SMn is linear.
         To determine whether the more general structures T V Bn , ST V Bn , and ST V Gn
         are linear, it is essential to construct and study their representations. This paper
         addresses this problem by exploring the complex local and homogeneous local rep-
         resentations of these generalized braid structures and examining their properties
         such as faithfulness and irreducibility.

             We begin in Section 2 by presenting the main definitions and propositions needed
         throughout the paper. In Section 3, we study all complex local representations of
         T V B2 and determine whether they are faithful and irreducible. This section also
         introduces complex homogeneous local representations of T V Bn for n ≥ 3 and
         analyzes their key properties. Section 4 focuses on complex local representations
         of ST V B2 , evaluating their faithfulness and irreducibility and posing several open
         questions for future work. Finally, in Section 5, we define Φ-type extensions of
         complex local representations of T V B2 to ST V B2 and discuss their structural and
         representational implications.

               It is observed that the monoid SMn is embedded in SBn , and the monoid
           ST V Bn is embedded in ST V Gn . A thorough understanding of the representations
           of these groups is paramount for assessing their linearity—that is, whether they
           admit faithful representations into general linear groups. To lay the groundwork,
           we first define group representations and discuss their fundamental properties. Fol-
           lowing this, we explore several types of representations pertinent to our study. We
           then introduce the prominent Burau representation of the braid group Bn , detailing
           its definition and summarizing its key findings.

           Definition 2.1. [26] Let G be a group. A representation of G of degree n is a
           group homomorphism

                                                 ρ : G → GLn (C),

           where GLn (C) is the group of all invertible n × n matrices with complex entries.

           Definition 2.2. [26] A representation ρ : G → GLn (C) is said to be irreducible
           if there is no nontrivial proper subspace W ⊂ Cn such that ρ(g)(w) ∈ W for all
           g ∈ G, w ∈ W .

           Definition 2.3. [26] A representation ρ : G → GLn (C) is said to be faithful if it
           is injective; that is, ker(ρ) = {e}, where e is the identity element of G.

            In the following proposition, we introduce the concept of Φ-type extensions of
         representations of the braid group Bn to the singular braid monoid SMn , given by
         Bardakov, Chbili, and Kozlovskaya in [3].

         Proposition 2.9. Let ϕ : Bn → Gn be a representation of Bn to a group Gn and
         let K be a field with a, b, c ∈ K. Then, the map Φa,b,c : SMn → K[Gn ] which acts
         on the generators of SMn by the following rules
                                               Φa,b,c (σi±1 ) = ϕ(σi±1 ),

                           Φa,b,c (τi ) = aϕ(σi ) + bϕ(σi−1 ) + ce, i = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1,
         where e is a neutral element of Gn , defines a representation of SMn to K[Gn ].

             In [25], Prabhakar and Negi extended the concept of Φ-type extensions given in
         Proposition 2.9 to T V Bn . They proved, in a similar way, that if ϕ : T V Bn → Gn
         is a representation of T V Bn to a group Gn and K is a field with a, b, c ∈ K, then,
         the map Φa,b,c : ST V Bn → K[Gn ] which acts on the generators of ST V Bn by the
         rules
                    Φa,b,c (σi±1 ) = ϕ(σi±1 ), Φa,b,c (ρi ) = ϕ(ρi ), Φa,b,c (γj ) = ϕ(γj ) and

                  Φa,b,c (τi ) = aϕ(σi ) + bϕ(σi−1 ) + ce, i = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1, j = 1, 2, . . . , n,
         where e is a neutral element of Gn , defines a representation of ST V Bn to K[Gn ].

         6. Conclusion

         This study opens several avenues for future research, particularly concerning the
         extension of known braid group representations to the twisted singular virtual braid
         groups. Building upon Bardakov’s work in [3] regarding extensions of the Burau
         representation to SMn , a key direction is to investigate analogous extensions to
         ST V Bn for all n ≥ 2. This would necessitate carefully defining images for the
         ρ and γ generators such that the relations of ST V Bn are preserved. A crucial
         subsequent question is whether every such extension of the Burau representation
         to ST V Bn adheres to the Φ-type form observed in Bardakov’s findings.
            Similarly, inspired by Bardakov’s classification of extensions of the Lawrence-
         Krammer-Bigelow Representation (LKBR) of Bn to SMn for n = 3, 4, future work
         can explore corresponding extensions to ST V Bn . This would again involve the
         meticulous definition of images for the ρ and γ generators. A significant line of
         inquiry would then be to determine if all extensions of the LKBR to ST V Bn for
         n = 3, 4 also conform to the Φ-type structure established in Bardakov’s research.

           representation theory of twisted singular virtual braid groups.
